Guide · Deduction economics

The real cost of retailer deductions for CPG brands

Most CPG brands know deductions exist. Few know the full cost. This guide quantifies it at every revenue tier — the dollars lost, the labor burned, the disputes missed, and the root causes ignored.

For CPG operations, finance, and leadership teams selling through KeHE, UNFI, and other major distributors.

The headline number

5 – 15% of gross sales.

That is the range most CPG brands lose to retailer and distributor deductions — shortage claims, compliance fees, promotional chargebacks, and pricing adjustments. The exact number depends on your trade terms, compliance track record, product category, and whether anyone on your team is actively classifying which deductions are contractual and which are recoverable.

At $1M in gross wholesale revenue, that is $80K–$150K. At $5M, it is $350K–$600K. At $10M, it exceeds $600K–$1M in annual deductions. The dollar amount compounds with revenue, but the rate can be reduced with classification, prevention, and recovery.

Beyond the dollar

The five hidden costs of unmanaged deductions.

The deduction amount on your remittance is the visible cost. These five costs do not appear on any report — but they compound with every quarter you operate without classification.

An estimated 20 – 40 hours/month

Labor

Hours spent reconciling remittances, hunting for BOLs and PODs, matching deductions to POs, and manually filing disputes through K-Solve or UNFI Direct. At an estimated $40-$60/hour (fully loaded) for an operations team member, that is roughly $800-$2,400/month in deduction-related labor before a single dispute is filed.

Unrecoverable

Missed deadlines

KeHE enforces a 180-day dispute window through K-Solve. UNFI timelines vary by deduction type. Every disputable deduction that expires past its window is cash that cannot be recovered — regardless of how strong the evidence was. Brands without systematic deadline tracking routinely lose 30-50% of their disputable deductions to expiration.

Compounds quarterly

Root cause blindness

Without classification, the same operational breakdown — a recurring DC miscount, a carrier that consistently misses appointment windows, a labeling spec that triggers compliance fees — repeats quarter after quarter. You pay the same deduction four times a year instead of fixing the $200 root cause once.

30 – 90 days

Cash flow gap

Revenue is recognized at shipment but cash arrives after deductions. A brand that ships $500K in a month and has a 10% deduction rate will collect approximately $450K, 30-90 days later. The $50K gap is real — it affects payroll, inventory purchases, and vendor payments. Brands that forecast from shipped POs without deduction adjustment routinely overestimate near-term cash by 8-12%.

Unmeasurable but real

Opportunity cost

Every hour your operations or finance team spends hunting for evidence in email, cross-referencing portal exports, and reconstructing what happened on a PO from 60 days ago is an hour not spent on sales strategy, new distribution relationships, inventory optimization, or product development. For a 5-person CPG brand, deduction work can consume an estimated 10-15% of total operational capacity.

By stage of growth

What deductions look like at your stage.

The deduction problem is different at each stage of wholesale growth. The dollar amount increases with revenue, but the operational pattern changes too — from invisible to reactive to (eventually) preventive.

$500K – $1M

10 – 15%

New to wholesale

The first KeHE remittance arrives and the brand discovers that $10,000 in shipped POs became $8,500 in cash. No one on the team has seen a K-Solve export before. The 111 prefix codes are undocumented. The 180-day dispute window is unknown.

Impact

No classification. No disputes filed. The remittance is accepted at face value because there is no process to question it.

$1M – $5M

8 – 12%

Scaling

An ops person reconciles remittances in a spreadsheet weekly. Some disputes are filed through K-Solve, but many windows expire while the team is focused on newer orders. The deduction rate is known but the composition is not — the team treats every deduction as a single category rather than separating contractual from disputable.

Impact

Partial recovery. Manual classification. Significant leakage from expired windows and unclassified deductions. an estimated 20-40 hours/month in deduction-related labor.

$5M – $30M

5 – 10%

Established

A dedicated operations person manages deductions. They know the KeHE portal, file disputes regularly, and have a working relationship with the broker. But the process is reactive: deductions are investigated after they post, evidence is gathered after the fact, and recurring patterns surface only when someone notices them.

Impact

Better recovery rate. But still reactive, not preventive. The deduction rate is lower partly because the brand's trade terms are more favorable, not only because the recovery process is better.

The split that matters

88% contractual. 12% disputable.

In one verified audit of $151,671 in KeHE deductions, 88% ($140,914) was contractual passthrough — charges that follow agreed trade terms. These are not errors. Disputing them wastes time and credibility.

The remaining 12% contained $10,757 in disputable items and an estimated $5,940 in recoverable value. That is the money that was taken incorrectly — and can be recovered with evidence and timely filing.

The real cost of unmanaged deductions is not the 88%. It is the disputable deductions your team never classified, the labor spent reviewing contractual charges that needed no action, and the recurring causes that went undetected because no one separated the signal from the noise.

Calculate yours

Your deduction rate in one formula.

Pull your last 90 days of remittance data. Sum the deduction dollars. Divide by gross invoiced. That is your trailing deduction rate.

Track it monthly. A sudden spike in compliance fees means something changed in your fulfillment. A drift upward in unknown items means your classification is falling behind.

The compound effect

Unmanaged deductions compound. Managed deductions shrink.

The difference between a brand that classifies, disputes, and prevents deductions and one that accepts remittances at face value grows wider every quarter. Classification reveals recurring causes. Disputes recover cash. Prevention stops the same causes from repeating. Each layer reduces the deduction rate, and the savings compound.

Year 1

Classify

Separate contractual from disputable. Stop reviewing the 88% that needs no action. Focus labor on the 12% that matters.

Year 2

Recover

Disputable deductions are caught, evidenced, and filed within the window. Recovery rate rises. Labor per deduction drops.

Year 3

Prevent

Recurring root causes identified and fixed upstream. Compliance fees decline. The deduction rate itself starts shrinking.

Frequently asked

What percentage of gross sales do CPG brands typically lose to deductions?+

Between 5% and 15% for brands selling through KeHE, UNFI, or other major distributors. The rate depends on trade terms, compliance track record, product category, and whether the brand actively classifies and disputes deductions. New-to-wholesale brands tend toward the higher end; established brands with active deduction management trend lower.

Are all deductions losses?+

No. In one verified audit, 88% of deduction dollars were contractual passthrough — charges that follow agreed terms (promotional allowances, MCB fees, CN pass-throughs). These are costs of doing business, not errors. The real loss is in the remaining 12%: disputable deductions that go unclassified, and the labor spent reviewing contractual deductions that need no action.

How much can I actually recover?+

Recovery potential varies by deduction mix. In one audit: $151,671 total deductions → $140,914 contractual (88%) → $10,757 disputable → $5,940 estimated recoverable. The recoverable dollars are real but typically smaller than the total deduction number. The larger savings come from preventing deductions upstream and reducing the labor to manage them.

Is deduction management worth it for a brand under $1M?+

Yes — but the approach differs. At sub-$1M revenue, a full-time hire is not justified. A free audit tool (like the EasyOps KeHE audit) can classify your deductions in 5 minutes and show whether you have disputable dollars. If the answer is yes, the audit has already paid for itself. The question is not whether to manage deductions, but which tool fits your scale.

How do I calculate my deduction rate?+

Total deduction dollars ÷ gross invoiced dollars × 100. Track it as a trailing 90-day rate by category (contractual, compliance, disputable, unknown). A sudden spike in compliance fees means something changed in your fulfillment. A drift upward in unknown items means your classification is falling behind.
