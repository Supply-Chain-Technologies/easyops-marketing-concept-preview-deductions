Guide · Deduction prevention

The best deduction is the one that never posts.

For CPG operations teams shipping through KeHE, UNFI, and other distributors.

Recovery gets the dollar back. Prevention keeps it from leaving. This guide covers the operational checkpoints, compliance rules, and system connections that prevent the most common CPG deductions before they hit your remittance.

The economics

Recovery costs money. Prevention saves it.

Every deduction your team recovers costs labor (research and filing), time (days to weeks per dispute), and potentially 20-35% of the recovered dollar if you use a recovery service. A deduction prevented costs a compliance check before shipment — and keeps the full dollar.

Recovery path

$250 fill-rate fee

  • Fee posts to remittance → someone notices it → matches to PO
  • Hunts for BOL, ASN, carrier records across systems → assembles packet
  • Files through K-Solve → waits ~3 weeks for resolution
  • If using a service: you keep $163-$200 of the $250

Total cost: $50-87 in service fees + hours of labor + 3-week delay

Prevention path

$250 fill-rate fee

  • Pre-shipment check flags: pick ticket shows 184 of 200 cases
  • Ops team expedites remaining 16 cases or files planned partial via EDI 860
  • Shipment arrives at 100% fill rate. No fee posts. No dispute needed.

Total cost: one pre-shipment check. You keep $250.

This example uses a fill-rate fee. The same economics apply to every preventable deduction type: appointment no-shows ($250), pallet violations ($75-$400), labeling non-compliance (3% of affected product), and routing violations. The cheapest deduction is the one you never have to recover.

Deduction types

The 7 most preventable deductions for CPG brands.

Not every deduction is preventable. Contractual passthrough — promotional allowances, MCB processing fees, weekly CN charges — follows agreed terms. These seven are compliance-based deductions that can be prevented with upstream checks, evidence collection, and system monitoring.

📊

Fill rate penalties

What causes it

Cases received at the DC fall below 95% of cases ordered. The gap may be a real shortage, a DC miscount, or an ASN mismatch.

How to prevent it

Confirm physical case counts match the PO before pickup. Reconcile pick ticket, BOL, and ASN quantities. If shipping partial, file a planned short-ship (EDI 860) in advance so the DC expects the reduced quantity.

Evidence to collect

Pick confirmation, BOL with carrier signature, ASN (EDI 856) with lot-level detail, pre-shipment pallet photos.

Fee: 3% fee on the shortage amount when fill rate drops below 95%.

🚛

Late delivery / OTD penalties

What causes it

On-time delivery falls below 85% for the quarter. Causes include missed appointments, carrier delays, reschedules, and traffic — but the penalty applies to the brand regardless of the cause.

How to prevent it

Schedule appointments 7+ days in advance through C3 Reservations. Confirm carrier pickup on the scheduled date. Monitor in-transit events. If a delay is unavoidable, reschedule the appointment before the delivery window closes.

Evidence to collect

C3 Reservations appointment history showing scheduled vs. actual delivery, carrier tracking events proving on-time departure, communication trail showing DC-initiated changes.

Fee: $250 per late PO when quarterly OTD falls below 85%.

⚡

ASN / EDI timing violations

What causes it

EDI 855 (PO acknowledgment) not sent within 24 hours of the 850. EDI 753 (ready to ship) not sent 3 business days before pickup. EDI 856 (ASN) missing or inaccurate.

How to prevent it

Automate EDI acknowledgment to fire within hours, not days. Set a calendar trigger for the 753 deadline. Validate ASN data against the BOL before transmission.

Evidence to collect

EDI transmission timestamps from SPS Commerce, Crstl, or TrueCommerce. System-generated acknowledgment records.

Fee: No direct fee for late 855, but it blocks the EDI 810 invoice. Late 753 can delay carrier dispatch and cascade into an OTD penalty.

📅

Appointment no-shows

What causes it

Carrier does not arrive at the DC during the scheduled appointment window. Causes include carrier dispatch failures, traffic, wrong DC, and scheduling errors.

How to prevent it

Confirm the appointment in C3 Reservations 7 days out. Verify the carrier has the correct DC address, date, and time. Monitor carrier dispatch the day before. Cancel appointments at least 24 hours in advance if the shipment will not be ready.

Evidence to collect

C3 Reservations booking confirmation, carrier dispatch records, cancellation receipts if applicable.

Fee: $250 per no-show. Same-day cancellation: $250.

🏷

Labeling / barcode non-compliance

What causes it

Case barcode scan rate falls below 95% at the DC. Unreadable, incorrect, or missing GTIN barcodes trigger the non-compliance threshold.

How to prevent it

Test barcode scan rates before shipping. Verify GTIN compliance through IX-ONE imaging. A single unreadable barcode format can pull the entire SKU below the 95% threshold — fix the master, not just the batch.

Evidence to collect

IX-ONE imaging reports, internal barcode scan test results, corrective action records.

Fee: 3% non-compliance fee on affected product when scan rate drops below 95%.

📦

Pallet / case-pack violations

What causes it

Non-standard pallets (not GMA 48x40), pinwheeled loads, floor-loaded trailers, overweight or overheight pallets, or missing PO placards.

How to prevent it

Use GMA-standard 48x40 pallets with 4-way entry. Verify weight (max 2,500 lb) and height (max 72 inches including pallet). Placard every pallet with the KeHE PO number and destination DC. Never pinwheel or floor-load unless explicitly agreed.

Evidence to collect

Pre-shipment pallet photos with timestamps, weight tickets, placard verification records.

Fee: Pinwheeled pallets: $75. Floor-loaded trailers: $400. Sort & segregate: $85 / $110 / $160+ depending on severity.

🌡

Routing guide violations

What causes it

Temperature out of spec at delivery, incorrect wrapping or stacking, non-compliant packaging, or failure to follow DC-specific requirements.

How to prevent it

Check temperature bands before loading: refrigerated 34-40 degrees F, proteins 28-40 degrees F, frozen -20 to 0 degrees F. Follow DC-specific stacking and wrapping rules. Document pre-load temperatures with timestamped photos or data logger readings.

Evidence to collect

Temperature logs (continuous from pickup through delivery), pre-load temperature photos, packaging compliance photos, data logger readings.

Fee: Temperature non-compliance risks full load rejection, not just a fee. Stacking and wrapping violations trigger extra handling charges.

Fee amounts are from the current KeHE IBR Fee Schedule and P&P Fee Schedule. Always verify against the current distributor documentation. For the complete fee reference, see the KeHE compliance guide.

Pre-shipment checklist

8 checks before every shipment.

Run these eight checks before every outbound shipment. Each one prevents a specific category of deduction and collects evidence that serves double duty: if a deduction still posts despite compliance, the evidence is already assembled for the dispute.

1

EDI 855 sent within 24 hours

PO acknowledged, invoice pathway open.

2

Item master verified (UPC, case pack, description)

No receiving discrepancies from data mismatches.

3

Inventory allocated and confirmed

No surprise short-ships. Planned partials filed via EDI 860.

4

EDI 753 sent 3 business days before pickup

Carrier dispatch on schedule.

5

Appointment booked in C3 Reservations (7+ days)

DC expecting the load. No no-show risk.

6

Pallet specs verified (GMA, weight, height, placards)

No handling fees at the dock.

7

Barcode scan rate tested (95%+ threshold)

No non-compliance fee on the SKU.

8

Temperature and packaging documented pre-load

Evidence collected before the carrier takes possession.

Prevention meets recovery

The evidence you collect to prevent deductions is the evidence that wins disputes.

Prevention and recovery are not separate workflows. They share the same evidence trail. The BOL you photograph before carrier handoff prevents a shortage dispute — and if a UDR still arrives, that same BOL is the decisive evidence for the dispute. The difference is timing: collect it proactively, not reactively.

Collect before shipment

BOL, pallet photos, temperature readings, barcode test results, ASN data. These take minutes to gather at the warehouse. The same evidence costs hours to reconstruct from scattered systems after a deduction posts.

Available for both paths

If the shipment is compliant, the evidence proves it. If a deduction posts anyway, the evidence is already assembled for the dispute. Either way, the work was done once.

Feeds root-cause prevention

Recurring deductions at the same DC, same carrier, or same SKU reveal systemic issues. The pattern is visible only when evidence is collected consistently across every shipment.

The prevention advantage

Most deduction tools start after the deduction posts.

Recovery services, point tools, and even internal teams typically begin when the remittance arrives with money missing. By then, the evidence trail is cold, the dispute window is ticking, and recovery is the only option.

EasyOps starts at the purchase order and works forward. Compliance is checked before shipment. Evidence is collected during fulfillment. When a deduction does post, the dispute is ready before someone even notices the charge. That is the structural difference between recovery-first and prevention-first deduction management.

Frequently asked

Can I prevent all retailer deductions?+

No. Some deductions are contractual passthrough — promotional allowances, MCB processing fees, weekly CN charges — and cannot be prevented because they follow agreed terms. Prevention applies to compliance-based deductions: shortages, late deliveries, EDI violations, labeling issues, and routing failures. In one audit, 88% of deduction dollars were contractual. The remaining 12% is where prevention matters most.

What is the most common preventable deduction for CPG brands?+

Fill rate penalties. When cases received at the DC fall below 95% of cases ordered, a 3% fee applies. The most common causes are DC miscounts (preventable with pre-shipment evidence), ASN mismatches (preventable with data validation), and unplanned short-ships (preventable with inventory confirmation before acknowledgment).

How does preventing deductions reduce recovery costs?+

A prevented deduction costs nothing. A recovered deduction costs labor (your team researching and filing), time (days to weeks per dispute), and potentially 20-35% of the recovered dollar to a recovery service. If you prevent a $500 fill-rate fee, you keep $500. If you recover it through a service, you keep $325-$400. The economics are straightforward: prevention is always cheaper than recovery.

Does EasyOps check UNFI compliance too?+

KeHE compliance checking is production-ready with validated rules for all major fee categories. UNFI compliance checking is actively in development. The architecture is distributor-agnostic — the compliance rules are distributor-specific, and UNFI rules are being mapped to the same checking framework. See the compliance guide for current KeHE coverage.

Where does prevention fit in the deduction management workflow?+

Prevention is the first step, not a separate workflow. The same systems that collect evidence for disputes — BOLs, ASN timestamps, appointment records, carrier tracking — also power the pre-shipment compliance checks. The difference is timing: check before the truck moves, not after the deduction posts.
