Guide · Deduction software

How to evaluate deduction recovery software for your CPG brand

Not all deduction tools work the same way. This guide covers what to evaluate, how the main approaches differ, and where each fits in a CPG operations workflow.

For CPG operations, finance, and leadership teams evaluating how to manage retailer and distributor deductions.

Direct answer

Three approaches. Each with tradeoffs.

CPG brands manage deductions through some combination of three approaches: an outsourced recovery service, a software tool, or an internal hire. Most brands end up with a hybrid, but the mix depends on volume, team size, distributor complexity, and how much of the deduction lifecycle you want to own versus outsource.

Recovery service

An outsourced provider that manages deductions on your behalf, typically charging 20-35% of recovered dollars.

Strengths

  • Zero effort from your team
  • Dedicated dispute specialists
  • No upfront cost

Tradeoffs

  • Incentive misalignment: they profit from your deductions continuing
  • Only pursue above-threshold deductions
  • No prevention — recovery only
  • You lose visibility into your own data

Point tool / SaaS software

Upload-based or API-connected software that classifies deductions, tracks disputes, and may file them automatically. Flat fee or per-PO pricing.

Strengths

  • Immediate time to value
  • Scales without headcount
  • Full visibility into your data
  • Platform fee — aligned incentives

Tradeoffs

  • Capability varies widely between vendors
  • Some classify but don't file
  • Evidence collection depth varies
  • May require system integration work

Internal hire / manual process

A full-time operations or finance team member who reconciles deductions, files disputes, and manages relationships with distributors.

Strengths

  • Human judgment on edge cases
  • Relationship with distributor reps
  • Deep institutional knowledge

Tradeoffs

  • Expensive ($60K-$90K+ fully loaded)
  • Doesn't scale with volume
  • Single point of failure
  • Context lost when they leave

Market landscape

How the deduction management market breaks down.

Several vendors serve different segments of the CPG deduction management market. Each has genuine strengths and real tradeoffs. The right choice depends on your revenue stage, team size, distributor mix, and whether you want to own the workflow or outsource it.

Glimpse

End-to-end deduction management combining software and a managed-service team. Raised $35M Series A. Claims 200+ customers and 20+ retailer integrations.

Best for

Brands that want deduction recovery handled for them, not by them. Active promotions target both enterprise ($100M+ revenue) and emerging ($1M–$30M) brands.

Strongest advantage

Combines technology with human analysts. 11 published case studies with named customers and specific dollar amounts. Strong proof density.

Where it falls short

Managed-service model means the brand builds no internal capability. Revenue is tied to deductions continuing — the same incentive structure as traditional recovery services.

OverDeduct

Free deduction audit tool plus self-serve software plus a contingency recovery service. Targets emerging CPG brands.

Best for

Brands wanting a free starting point. Their comparison content ranks well in search for evaluation-intent queries.

Strongest advantage

Free audit entry point with low friction. Covers both KeHE and UNFI deductions. Clear pricing for the software tier.

Where it falls short

Starts at the deduction, not the PO. No upstream prevention, no systems integration with EDI/WMS/email, and no evidence assembly from fulfillment.

iNymbus

Automated deduction management and dispute filing. Enterprise-oriented platform covering multiple retailers.

Best for

Large CPG companies ($50M+ revenue) with high deduction volume that need scale automation for dispute filing.

Strongest advantage

Scale automation for high-volume dispute filing. Strong content marketing for KeHE K-Solve how-to queries.

Where it falls short

Enterprise pricing and implementation timeline. Not designed for emerging or scaling CPG brands ($500K–$5M revenue). Prevention is not the primary focus.

HighRadius

ERP-adjacent accounts receivable platform with deduction management as one module in a broader AR automation suite.

Best for

Enterprise organizations with complex ERP environments (SAP, Oracle) that want deduction management integrated into their existing AR workflow.

Strongest advantage

Deep ERP integration for enterprise. Mature platform with broad AR automation beyond just deductions.

Where it falls short

Not CPG-specific. Deductions are one feature among many. KeHE/UNFI-specific depth is limited compared to specialized tools.

Confido

Deduction management platform with OCR-based intake and categorization. Originally focused on Costco and Target retailer deductions.

Best for

Brands managing deductions from large retailers (Costco, Target, Walmart) where backup PDF scanning and categorization is the primary need.

Strongest advantage

Clean interface. Strong at categorizing deductions from scanned backup PDFs. Good for retailer-direct deduction workflows.

Where it falls short

KeHE-specific depth is limited — does not classify at the level of 111 prefix codes. No PO-level operational context or upstream prevention.

EasyOps is purpose-built for emerging and scaling CPG brands ($500K–$30M revenue) selling through KeHE and UNFI. It starts at the purchase order and works forward — prevention before recovery. Before the demo

Six questions to ask any deduction software vendor.

These questions separate vendors who have depth from those who have a slide deck. Bring your own deduction data to the call — the answers are more honest when they're running against your numbers.

“What's your classification accuracy, and how do you validate it?”

A vendor that can't cite a specific number with a defined validation methodology is guessing. Ask for the denominator: "100% accuracy on what? 10 codes or 100?"

“Do you file disputes, or just identify them?”

Identification without filing creates a to-do list, not a recovery. Ask whether the tool submits directly to the distributor portal or produces a report you file manually.

“What happens after recovery — does the root cause get fixed?”

Recovery gets the dollar back. Prevention keeps it from leaving. Ask whether the tool surfaces recurring patterns (by DC, by cause, by time period) that feed upstream operational changes.

“How are you priced — by recovered dollar, by PO, or flat fee?”

Percentage-of-recovered creates a misaligned incentive: the provider profits from your deductions continuing. A platform fee aligns the provider's interest with reducing your deductions over time.

“Which distributors do you support, and how deep?”

"We support KeHE" can mean anything from "we read K-Solve exports" to "we have validated rules for every deduction code." Ask for the code-level coverage, not just the distributor name.

“Can I see my own data during the evaluation?”

The strongest signal is whether the vendor will run your actual deduction data during the demo, not just show a canned demo environment.

The practical answer

Most brands end up with a combination.

Software handles the recurring classification, evidence collection, and dispute filing. A person makes judgment calls, manages distributor relationships, and handles edge cases. A service may add specialized execution for a defined scope. The evaluation should begin with which jobs are recurring (automate), which require judgment (staff), and which are currently being abandoned (tool or service).

Start by understanding what your team is actually doing today — not what a vendor slide deck says they should be doing. Then evaluate which approach fills the biggest gap with the least friction.

Frequently asked

What's the difference between a recovery service and deduction management software?+

A recovery service is an outsourced provider that manages disputes for you, typically taking 20-35% of what they recover. Deduction management software gives your team the tools to classify, dispute, and prevent deductions directly, usually for a flat platform fee. The key difference is who owns the workflow and how the economics align.

Is EasyOps a recovery service?+

No. EasyOps is a deduction management platform. It classifies deductions, assembles evidence, files disputes through distributor portals, and checks compliance before shipment. The pricing is a platform fee — not a percentage of recovered dollars. Your team owns the workflow and retains full visibility.

How much does deduction management software cost?+

Pricing varies widely. Recovery services charge 20-35% of recovered dollars, which can be significant at scale. SaaS tools typically charge a monthly platform fee ranging from hundreds to thousands per month depending on volume. Internal hires cost $60K-$90K+ fully loaded. The right comparison is total cost of ownership, including the time your team spends on deductions regardless of which approach you use.

Can I use multiple tools together?+

Yes. Many CPG brands use a combination: software for classification and evidence, a recovery service for above-threshold disputes they don't have time to file, and an internal person for judgment calls and relationship management. The evaluation should focus on which jobs are recurring (automate), which require judgment (staff), and which are currently being abandoned (tool or service).

How do I compare deduction tools?+

Start with the seven dimensions in the evaluation framework above: classification accuracy, evidence automation, dispute filing, prevention, distributor coverage, pricing model, and time to value. Weight them based on your specific situation — a brand new to KeHE cares most about classification accuracy; a brand at scale cares about automation and prevention.

How does EasyOps compare to Glimpse?+

Glimpse combines software with a managed-service team — they handle deductions for you. EasyOps is a platform — your team handles deductions with software that classifies, assembles evidence, files disputes, and checks compliance before shipment. Glimpse is stronger if you want someone else to own the workflow. EasyOps is stronger if you want to build internal capability, prevent deductions upstream, and retain full visibility into your data. The pricing models also differ: Glimpse's managed service involves a share of recovered dollars; EasyOps charges a flat platform fee.

Is there a free deduction audit tool?+

Yes. EasyOps offers a free deduction audit at compliance-audit.tryeasyops.ai — upload a KeHE K-Solve export and see which lines are contractual versus disputable in about 5 minutes. No sign-up required. OverDeduct also offers a free audit tool. Both are useful starting points for understanding your deduction landscape before committing to a platform or service.
