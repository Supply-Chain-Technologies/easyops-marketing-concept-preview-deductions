Field guide · KeHE evidence

The KeHE deduction evidence checklist

What to collect for each deduction type, organized by cause — not by document type, not by prefix code.

Different deductions require different evidence. A signed BOL wins a shortage dispute but means nothing for a pricing dispute. This checklist maps each cause to the specific documents KeHE expects.

AuthorJordan Harper, CEO, EasyOps

Last reviewedSeptember 9, 2026

Direct answer

The evidence depends on the cause, not the deduction code.

KeHE uses 111 prefix codes, but evidence requirements group into seven cause categories. A shortage dispute needs proof of what shipped and what arrived. A pricing dispute needs the governing agreement and the PO. An EDI compliance charge needs transmission timestamps. Collecting the right evidence for the wrong cause wastes time and weakens the dispute.

Each section below shows one cause, the prefix codes it covers, and the specific documents to collect. Check the source reference for the governing KeHE policy section.

Evidence by cause

Seven causes. Seven different evidence packets.

Each card below shows the deduction cause, the prefix codes it covers, and the specific documents to collect. The checklist items are ordered by importance.

01Shortage / quantity variance

IA, KC

  • Signed BOL (proof what left the dock)
  • Pick confirmation / pack list (proof what was picked)
  • Proof of delivery / POD (proof what arrived)
  • UDR response (must be within 48 hours per P&P §6.6)
  • Prior IA on same PO (double-deduction check — near-100% win rate)

DC case count governs. If the BOL, pick confirmation, and POD all agree and KeHE’s count differs, the evidence packet is strong.

Source: P&P §6.7 — DC case count governs

02Late delivery / appointment

FF

  • C3 Reservations appointment history
  • Carrier tracking events
  • Communication showing who moved the appointment
  • Check: is the fee appointment-class (both methods) or lumping-class (Supplier Delivered only)?

Late fees vary by delivery method. Appointment-class fees apply to both KeHE Delivered and Supplier Delivered. Lumping fees apply only to Supplier Delivered.

Source: IBR Fee Schedule p.2; P&P §6.3

03Fill rate / service level

SLF

  • Fill rate report from KeHE
  • Shipment records showing actual quantity shipped
  • BOL confirming full load
  • Escalation path: Supplier Manager per Understanding Your Financial Account p.18

KeHE assesses 3% of the shortage value when fill rate drops below 95%. If your records show full shipment, the DC miscount is your evidence.

Source: P&P §6.5 — 95% threshold, 3% fee

04Damage / spoils

G, Q

  • Photos at handoff to carrier
  • POD notations about condition
  • Temperature logs (for temp-sensitive)
  • Chain of custody documentation
  • Distinguish: G = physical damage, Q = date-code failure, CS = fixed-rate allowance

Physical damage (G) requires evidence at the carrier handoff. Date-code failure (Q) is about shelf life at receipt. CS is a proactive deposit — not event-driven — and is typically contractual.

Source: P&P §9.2, §9.3; IBR Guide §5–§7

05Pricing / allowance

IA, MCB

  • Governing agreement / deal sheet
  • PO with pricing detail
  • Invoice
  • Prior deductions under same term
  • For MCB: check AAP enrollment (AAP-enrolled = exempt from processing fee)

Pricing disputes require the written agreement as the anchor. If the PO price, invoice, and deal sheet all agree and the deduction differs, the packet is strong. MCB disputes should check AAP status first.

Source: P&P §4, §7.4; Fee Schedule

06EDI / ASN compliance

EDI-related codes

  • 855 transmission timestamp (due within 24h of PO)
  • 753 transmission timestamp (due 3 business days before pickup)
  • 856 transmission timestamp
  • Acknowledgement records from SPS Commerce or EDI provider

The 855 deadline is the one suppliers most often discover after missing it. Transmission timestamps from your EDI provider are the primary evidence.

Source: IBR Guide

07Retailer-specific pass-through

CN, SF, GP, etc.

  • Retailer program agreement
  • Trailing 4-week average for anomaly detection
  • DC shipping records to verify the charge maps to actual activity
  • Retailer buyer confirmation if anomalous

Pass-throughs are charges KeHE forwards from the end retailer. Most are contractual, but anomalous spikes should be verified against actual shipping activity.

Source: P&P §8

Current KeHE source documents, agreements, and portal instructions govern deadlines, channels, and requirements.

When to collect

Before shipment vs. after deduction.

Evidence collection happens in two modes. Proactive collection builds the packet before a deduction ever posts. Reactive collection assembles it after the charge appears. Proactive is faster, more complete, and wins more disputes.

PROACTIVE

Collect at the event

  • At pick:Save the pick confirmation and pack list
  • At shipment:Attach the signed BOL and ASN transmission timestamp
  • At delivery:Capture the POD, photos, and temperature logs
  • At appointment:Screenshot the C3 Reservations confirmation
  • At invoicing:Archive the PO, invoice, and deal sheet together
REACTIVE

Collect after the deduction

  • Day 1:Identify the cause and check the checklist above
  • Day 2–5:Request missing documents from 3PL, carrier, or EDI provider
  • Day 5–10:Assemble the complete packet and verify each item
  • Day 10–14:File through K-Solve with all evidence attached
  • Ongoing:Track response — standard SLA is approximately 3 weeks

The 180-day clock

Evidence becomes harder to find as time passes.

The 180-day dispute window is a deadline, not a target. Every day between the event and the dispute makes evidence harder to collect. Carrier records age out. Warehouse staff rotate. Photos get deleted. Communication threads get buried.

1

Week 1

All evidence is fresh. BOLs are signed, PODs are filed, carrier tracking is live, photos are on the driver’s phone, and warehouse staff remember the shipment. This is when proactive collection captures everything.

2

Week 2–4

Carrier tracking events start archiving. 3PL systems rotate logs. Communication threads move off the first page. Still recoverable, but requires deliberate requests.

3

Month 2–3

Warehouse staff may not remember the specific shipment. Temperature logs may have been overwritten. Photos from the loading dock are harder to locate. Reactive collection takes significantly more effort.

4

Month 4–6

You are now in the back half of the dispute window. Evidence gaps are difficult to close. If the BOL or POD was not captured at the event, it may be unrecoverable. The dispute must be filed before day 180 regardless.

Collect at the event, not at the dispute. The strongest evidence packets are assembled before the deduction posts.

Frequently asked

Why organize evidence by cause instead of by document type?+

Because different deduction types require different evidence. A signed BOL is decisive for a shortage dispute but irrelevant for a pricing dispute. Organizing by cause ensures you collect exactly what KeHE expects for that specific charge — nothing missing, nothing wasted.

What is the most important document for a shortage dispute?+

The signed BOL, because it proves what left the dock. When the BOL, pick confirmation, and POD all agree on the same quantity and KeHE’s DC count differs, the evidence packet is strong. If a prior IA already exists on the same PO, flag it as a double-deduction — near-100% win rate.

How long do I have to collect evidence after a deduction posts?+

Disputes must be filed through K-Solve within 180 days of the deduction date. But evidence becomes harder to find as time passes — carrier records age out, warehouse staff rotate, photos get deleted. The strongest packets are assembled at the event, not after the deduction posts.

What if I am missing a key document?+

Do not file without it. A dispute with incomplete evidence is weaker than a dispute filed a few days later with the right documents. Request the missing piece from your 3PL, carrier, or EDI provider, then file with a complete packet.

Where does EasyOps fit?+

The Intelligent PO™ workspace attaches evidence to the order as it happens — BOL at shipment, POD at delivery, ASN timestamps at transmission. When a deduction posts, the packet already exists. The free compliance audit identifies which deductions are disputable and what evidence you need for each.

Evidence boundary: This guide is an operating framework, not a substitute for current KeHE documents or a determination that any specific deduction is recoverable. Always verify current terms, evidence, and deadlines.
