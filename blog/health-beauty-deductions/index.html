Industry guide · Health, Beauty & Personal Care

Stop absorbing damage and pricing deductions you didn't earn

Natural HBA and personal-care brands shipping through KeHE and UNFI face a deduction profile driven by fragile packaging, strict pricing programs, and new-item velocity. Damage claims, MAP charges, and slotting fees pile up. This guide maps each type, the evidence that wins disputes, and how to prevent the avoidable ones.

For CPG operations and finance teams managing health, beauty, and personal care deductions.

Direct answer

Three deduction drivers unique to health and beauty brands.

Every CPG category faces fill-rate fees and compliance charges. HBA brands face those plus a category-specific trio: damage and concealed-shortage claims from fragile packaging, MAP and pricing deductions from complex promotional programs, and new-item and slotting fees from aggressive launch velocity. The damage problem alone can represent 2-5% of gross sales for brands shipping glass, pumps, or aerosols.

The good news: all three are among the most disputable deduction types when the evidence is assembled correctly. Concealed-damage claims without receiving notations, pricing charges that contradict the active deal sheet, and slotting fees charged outside the agreement all have strong win rates.

Five deduction types

The deductions that shape the HBA P&L.

G-$418.60

Damage / concealed shortage

Trigger: Product damage reported at DC receiving or discovered after delivery. Fragile HBA packaging — glass bottles, pumps, aerosols — draws disproportionate damage claims.

Disputable? Often — especially concealed-damage claims where damage was not noted at receipt

Evidence: Pre-shipment photos, POD notations, ASN, carrier condition report, temperature logs

IA-$275.00

MAP / pricing mismatch

Trigger: Invoice cost differs from the agreed deal-sheet price. Common when promotional pricing is mis-keyed, MAP programs change mid-cycle, or cost updates are not synchronized.

Disputable? Usually — traces directly to a document (deal sheet, PO, promotional agreement)

Evidence: Signed deal sheet, PO with agreed price, promotional calendar, invoice

Various-$500.00

New-item / slotting / free-fill

Trigger: Introductory allowance, slotting fee, or free-fill requirement for new items entering distribution. Often negotiated but mis-applied — charged outside the agreement or double-charged.

Disputable? Yes — when charged outside the agreement, duplicated, or applied to the wrong item

Evidence: New-item agreement, slotting contract, prior deductions under the same term

SLF-$196.35

Fill-rate fee

Trigger: Service level falls below 95%. Same mechanism as grocery and supplements. HBA brands see lower velocity than grocery, so the quarterly average is less volatile.

Disputable? Often — when DC miscount caused the shortfall

Evidence: Signed BOL, POD, pick confirmation

Various-$350–$800

Temperature compliance

Trigger: Cold-chain HBA products (certain probiotics, oils, serums) arrive outside the required temperature band. Less common than for supplements but high-stakes when it occurs.

Disputable? Only with continuous temperature logging

Evidence: Temperature data logger, pre-load temperature photo, carrier handoff records

The concealed-damage problem

Why HBA brands get disproportionate damage claims — and how to build the evidence trail.

Glass bottles crack. Pump mechanisms break. Aerosol cans dent. Natural HBA brands ship products that are beautiful on a shelf and fragile in a DC. The result is a persistent damage-deduction pattern that most brands accept because they cannot prove when the damage occurred. The fix is an evidence trail that starts before the carrier takes possession.

1

Pre-shipment pallet photos

Timestamped photos of every pallet before the carrier takes possession. Shows product condition at the point of custody transfer. Without these, you have no counter-evidence to a damage claim.

2

Pallet wrapping and protection documentation

HBA brands should use reinforced wrapping for glass, pumps, and aerosol products. Photograph the wrapping method. This shows the DC that fragile items were packed to prevent damage, shifting the burden toward transit or receiving.

3

Carrier condition report

The carrier's own record of product condition at pickup. If the carrier accepted the load without noting damage, the custody chain is clean through transit. Request this document for every delivery that results in a damage claim.

4

POD with receiving notations

The proof of delivery signed by the DC receiver. If no damage was noted at receipt but a damage claim appears later, this is a concealed-damage scenario — and concealed-damage claims are disputable because the chain of custody was unbroken.

5

Temperature data logger

For cold-chain HBA products: continuous temperature records from pickup through delivery. A logger showing product stayed within band for the entire transit proves the damage was not temperature-related.

For the full evidence requirements by deduction type, see the evidence playbook.

Pricing defense

Four controls that prevent MAP and pricing deductions.

HBA brands run promotional programs, MAP policies, and seasonal pricing changes that create pricing deductions when the systems are not synchronized. These four controls address the root cause.

1

Version-control every deal sheet

Every promotional agreement, MAP program, and cost change should be version-controlled with effective dates. When a pricing deduction lands, you need to prove which version was active for the PO in question — not the current version, the version that governed at the time of the order.

2

Verify PO price against the active deal sheet before shipping

Before the pick ticket prints, compare the PO price to the active deal sheet. A mismatch caught before shipment is a conversation. A mismatch caught on the remittance is a deduction.

3

Align promotional calendars with distributor timing

MAP and promotional pricing changes must be synchronized with the distributor's system. If you lower a price for a promotion but KeHE's system still has the old cost, the invoice will mismatch and trigger a pricing deduction. Confirm timing with your buyer before the promotion starts.

4

Track all prior deductions under the same term

When a pricing or allowance deduction repeats, check whether the same charge was already taken on a prior PO. Double-deductions under the same promotional term have strong win rates because the distributor's own records show the charge was already applied.

Frequently asked

Health & beauty deduction questions.

Why do HBA brands get more damage deductions than grocery?+

Packaging fragility. Glass bottles, pump dispensers, aerosol containers, and thin-walled tubes are all more susceptible to transit damage than a cardboard cereal box. Natural HBA brands also tend to use premium packaging materials that are attractive but less durable for distribution. The combination of fragile packaging and standard distribution handling creates a persistent damage-deduction pattern.

What is a concealed-damage claim and can I dispute it?+

A concealed-damage claim is a damage charge filed after delivery when no damage was noted on the proof of delivery at receipt. These are frequently disputable because the chain of custody was unbroken: the carrier delivered, the DC signed for it, and damage appeared later. The evidence is the pre-shipment photos showing intact product and the POD showing clean receipt.

How do MAP/pricing deductions happen?+

When the cost billed on the invoice differs from the price the distributor has on file — often from a mis-keyed promotional cost, a MAP change that was not synchronized, or a cost update that took effect on a different date than the PO. Because they trace directly to a document (deal sheet, PO, promotional agreement), they are usually disputable with the right paperwork.

Are new-item and slotting fees recoverable?+

They are negotiable and frequently mis-applied: charged outside the agreement, applied to the wrong item, or double-charged. EasyOps flags each against your new-item agreement so you only pay what was actually agreed. The dispute evidence is the signed agreement showing the original terms.

How does EasyOps help HBA brands specifically?+

Two primary ways. First, chain-of-custody evidence automation: pre-shipment photos, carrier handoff records, and POD notations attach to the PO automatically, so when a damage claim arrives, the evidence packet is already assembled. Second, deal-sheet matching: the system compares invoice prices to active promotional agreements and flags pricing deductions that don't match the governing document.

Evidence boundary: This guide is an operational reference for HBA brands. Damage policies, pricing processes, and new-item fee structures vary by distributor. Always verify current terms directly with each distributor.
