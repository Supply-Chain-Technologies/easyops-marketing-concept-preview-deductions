How to dispute a retailer deduction

For CPG operations and finance teams who need to contest a deduction from KeHE, UNFI, or any retailer — from the first remittance line through filed dispute and resolution.

Before you file

Not every deduction should be disputed.

In one verified audit, 88% of deduction dollars were contractual passthrough — charges that follow agreed terms. Disputing those wastes time and weakens your credibility with the distributor.

Is this deduction contractual or disputable?

Check it against your agreements, fee schedules, and distributor policies before spending time on evidence collection.

Do I have the evidence?

A dispute without the right evidence packet is worse than no dispute. Know what documents you need before you file.

Is the dispute window still open?

For KeHE: 180 days through K-Solve. For UNFI: varies by type. A strong dispute filed after the window closes is worth zero.

The dispute process

Seven steps from deduction line to resolution.

This process works for KeHE, UNFI, and direct retailer deductions. The portals and deadlines differ; the operating logic is the same.

01

Locate the deduction on your remittance or portal

Find the line on your remittance advice, K-Solve export, or UNFI Direct report. Preserve the original amount, date, deduction code, PO reference, and any description. Do not alter anything before you have a copy.

02

Match it to the purchase order

Connect the deduction line to the specific purchase order, invoice, shipment, and receiving event. An unmatched deduction is not ready for a dispute. The PO is the anchor for every piece of evidence downstream.

03

Classify: contractual or disputable?

Check the charge against your agreements, fee schedules, and distributor policies. In one verified audit, 88% of deduction dollars were contractual passthrough. Disputing contractual charges wastes capacity and erodes your credibility with the distributor.

04

Check the dispute deadline

KeHE requires disputes filed through K-Solve within 180 days of the deduction date. UNFI timelines vary by deduction category. Missing the window means the dollars are permanently unrecoverable regardless of the evidence.

05

Assemble the evidence packet

Gather the documents that speak to the specific cause of this deduction on this PO. Generic company documents will not win a dispute. The evidence must be PO-specific, date-specific, and cause-specific.

06

Draft and file the dispute

Write a clear dispute description referencing the PO, the deduction code, the amount, and the evidence attached. For KeHE, file through K-Solve. For UNFI, file through UNFI Direct. Email submissions are no longer accepted by most distributors.

07

Track to resolution and feed back the root cause

Record the outcome, response time, and owner. Standard response SLA is approximately 3 weeks. If the cause was operational — recurring shortages at a specific DC, timing violations, documentation gaps — route the finding into fulfillment changes so the deduction stops recurring.

Evidence quick reference

What evidence you need depends on the cause.

Different deduction types require different documents. The packet must be specific to the PO and the cause — generic evidence does not win disputes.

What wins

Four things that separate winning disputes from wasted effort.

1

Specific to the PO

The evidence references this purchase order, this shipment, this receiving event. A generic BOL template or company-wide pick report is not sufficient.

2

Evidence speaks to the cause

If the deduction is for a shortage, the evidence must show what was actually shipped and received. If it is for a late delivery, the evidence must show when the appointment was booked and when delivery occurred.

3

Filed inside the window

A strong dispute filed on day 181 is worth zero. Prioritize by deadline urgency, not just dollar value. A $200 dispute expiring next week matters more than a $2,000 dispute with five months remaining.

4

Double-deduction check

When a distributor takes an IA (at receiving) and then a KC (from a post-receipt audit) on the same PO for the same shortage, that is a double-deduction. These have a historically high win rate because the distributor's own records show the charge was taken twice.

Frequently asked

What evidence do I need to dispute a shortage deduction?+

A signed BOL, packing list, pick confirmation, and proof of delivery. These must be specific to the PO and shipment date. If an IA (invoice adjustment at receiving) was already taken and a KC (post-receipt audit) arrives later for the same PO, flag it as a double-deduction — these carry a historically high win rate.

How long do I have to dispute a KeHE deduction?+

KeHE requires disputes filed through K-Solve within 180 days of the deduction date. K-Solve quick search covers roughly the last 90 days; advanced search reaches back 2 years. UNFI timelines vary by deduction category and are generally shorter for some types. Current distributor documents govern the actual deadlines.

Can I dispute a deduction after the window closes?+

Generally no. Once the dispute window closes, the dollars are considered accepted by the distributor. This is why monitoring deadlines is critical. If you discover a systematic error (e.g., double-deductions across multiple POs), escalate to your distributor account manager — some exceptions exist, but they are not guaranteed.

What is a double-deduction and why does it always win?+

A double-deduction occurs when a distributor takes two charges on the same PO for the same event — typically an IA at receiving and a KC from a later internal audit. The win rate is historically high because the distributor's own system shows the charge was applied twice. Always check for prior deductions on the same PO before filing any dispute.
