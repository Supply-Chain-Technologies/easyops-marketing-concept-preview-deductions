KeHE vs. UNFI: how deduction management differs

Most CPG brands sell through both KeHE and UNFI simultaneously. That means two portals, two code systems, two dispute processes, and two sets of deadlines managed by the same small team. This post maps the operational differences and where the complexity actually lives.

For CPG operations, finance, and sales teams managing deductions across both KeHE and UNFI.

A note on coverage depth

We know KeHE deeply. We are learning UNFI.

EasyOps was built on KeHE data first. We have classified all 111 KeHE prefix codes, validated classification rules at 100% accuracy for 48 of them, and built an audit tool that has processed real supplier exports. Our KeHE coverage reflects thousands of hours of work with real deduction data.

UNFI coverage is earlier. We have mapped the portal structure, understand the dispute process at a working level, and are actively building classification rules for UNFI codes. Where this post says “different” or “own system” on the UNFI side, that is an honest statement that we have not yet documented it to the same depth. We will update this post as our UNFI expertise reaches the level of our KeHE work.

Every CPG brand we speak with manages both distributors. That dual-distributor reality is the reason this comparison exists, even when one side is more complete than the other.

Portals and disputes

Two portals. Two dispute processes. Zero shared interface.

KeHE and UNFI each maintain their own supplier portal. There is no interoperability, no shared login, and no common API for suppliers. Every action must be performed separately in each system.

Dispute portal

KeHE

K-Solve: supplier-facing deduction and dispute portal. Shows deduction data, reason codes, and is the only accepted channel for filing disputes.

UNFI

UNFI Direct: separate supplier portal with its own login, interface, and dispute submission process. Navigation and field layout differ from K-Solve.

Dispute window

KeHE

180 days from the deduction date. Strict cutoff. Email submissions are no longer accepted.

UNFI

Varies by deduction type. Timelines are generally shorter for some categories. Check current UNFI terms for exact windows.

Evidence format

KeHE

Portal-only submissions through K-Solve. Expects PO-specific evidence packages: BOLs, PODs, signed deal sheets, timestamped EDI records.

UNFI

Portal submissions through UNFI Direct. Evidence requirements vary by deduction category. Attachment formats and size limits differ from KeHE.

Deduction visibility

KeHE

K-Solve exports to Excel. Up to two years of historical transaction detail. Prefix codes identify deduction type.

UNFI

UNFI Direct provides deduction reporting. Different code system, different export format, different field naming conventions.

Fee structures

Different codes. Different rates. Different billing logic.

Both distributors charge fees for logistics, promotions, compliance, and fill-rate shortfalls. The rates, code systems, and billing cadence are entirely separate. A fee that is one line item at KeHE may appear as multiple entries at UNFI, or vice versa.

Code system

KeHE

111 distinct prefix codes. Each represents a different billing mechanism. EasyOps has validated classification rules for 48 codes at 100% accuracy.

UNFI

Own code taxonomy. Different naming conventions, different groupings. EasyOps is actively mapping UNFI codes but has not yet reached the same depth as KeHE.

Freight and logistics

KeHE

IBR (Inbound Receiving) fee schedule. Freight codes (FF prefix). Specific per-unit and per-case rates.

UNFI

Own freight and logistics fee structure. Different rate structures and billing cadence.

Promotional fees

KeHE

P&P (Promotions & Placement) fee schedule. Introductory allowances (IAA, 15% minimum), slotting (S), placement (P, IP, NP, PL).

UNFI

Own promotional fee structure. Different terminology for similar concepts. Trade spend mechanics differ in how they appear on remittance.

Fill-rate penalties

KeHE

SLF: Service Level Fine. 3% fee when fill rate drops below 95%.

UNFI

Own fill-rate penalty structure. Different thresholds and calculation methods.

Minimum charges

KeHE

MCB: Minimum Case Buy at 8%. Applies when order volume falls below threshold.

UNFI

Own minimum order requirements. Different calculation basis and percentage.

For the full KeHE code reference, see the 111 prefix codes hub. UNFI code mapping will be published as validation reaches parity.

Routing and compliance

Same warehouse. Different rulebooks.

Your 3PL ships to both KeHE and UNFI DCs from the same warehouse. But each distributor has its own scheduling system, pallet specifications, temperature requirements, and labeling rules. Non-compliance at either generates chargebacks.

Scheduling

KeHE

C3 Reservations: appointment scheduling for inbound loads. Specific booking windows and lead-time requirements.

UNFI

Own scheduling system with different booking process, lead times, and confirmation requirements.

Pallet requirements

KeHE

GMA pallets required. Specific stacking and wrapping standards per DC.

UNFI

Own pallet specifications. Requirements may differ by region and DC.

Temperature

KeHE

Specific temperature bands for dry, refrigerated, and frozen. Compliance is checked at receiving.

UNFI

Own temperature requirements. Band definitions and monitoring may differ.

Barcode and labeling

KeHE

GTIN compliance required. IX-ONE imaging. Gladson imaging for marketing content.

UNFI

Own barcode and labeling requirements. Different imaging and compliance verification processes.

For KeHE-specific compliance rules, see the KeHE compliance guide.

The dual-distributor problem

The complexity doubles. The team does not.

Every CPG brand that sells through both KeHE and UNFI experiences the same compounding problem. Two complete systems, each with full operational weight, managed by the same one to three people who also handle sales, forecasting, and broker coordination.

Two portals, two logins, two interfaces

K-Solve and UNFI Direct are completely separate systems. Different URLs, different credentials, different navigation. There is no shared view. An ops team managing both distributors Alt-Tabs between two portals, each with its own logic for finding the same type of information.

Two code systems, two classification languages

A shortage at KeHE is coded differently than a shortage at UNFI. The same operational event, such as a short shipment at receiving, generates deductions with different codes, different field names, and different reference structures. Classification rules that work for one distributor do not transfer to the other.

Two dispute processes, two deadlines

The 180-day window at KeHE is a hard cutoff. UNFI timelines differ by category. A team that misses a deadline at one distributor because they were focused on the other has lost recovery potential permanently. The calendaring burden alone is a workflow problem.

Two evidence standards, one warehouse

The BOL, POD, and packing list come from the same warehouse and carrier regardless of which distributor the order was for. But the format each distributor expects for dispute evidence differs. The same document may need to be packaged differently for each submission.

The team does not double

Most emerging CPG brands have one to three people managing both distributor relationships simultaneously. The operational complexity doubles when you add a second distributor, but headcount rarely follows. The work either gets slower, less thorough, or both.

Deduction types compared

Same deduction. Different codes. Different rules.

A shortage at KeHE and a shortage at UNFI are the same operational event — your shipment arrived short. But the code, the dispute window, the portal, and the evidence packaging are all different. Here is how the six most common deduction types compare across both distributors.

Shortage / short-ship

KeHE

IA (Invoice Adjustment) prefix codes. DC case count governs. UDR response within 48 hours.

UNFI

Own shortage codes through UNFI Direct. Different field names and reference structure.

Dispute window

KeHE: 180 days via K-Solve. UNFI: varies by category, generally shorter for some types.

Evidence

Same core evidence (BOL, POD, packing list). KeHE requires K-Solve portal submission. UNFI requires UNFI Direct with different attachment formats and size limits.

Late delivery / OTD

KeHE

FF prefix (freight/logistics). C3 Reservations appointment history. No-show fee: $250. Late delivery calculated on rolling quarterly OTD.

UNFI

Own scheduling system. Different booking process, lead times, and fee structure for late deliveries.

Dispute window

KeHE: 180 days. UNFI: check current terms for specific OTD dispute windows.

Evidence

KeHE: C3 Reservations records + carrier tracking. UNFI: own appointment system records. Carrier tracking is the same evidence, packaging differs.

Fill-rate penalty

KeHE

SLF (Service Level Fine): 3% fee when fill rate drops below 95%. Calculated on shortage amount.

UNFI

Own fill-rate penalty structure. Different threshold percentages and calculation methods.

Dispute window

KeHE: 180 days. UNFI: varies.

Evidence

Both require BOL and ASN proving shipped quantities. The PO and receiving count are the anchor. Same warehouse records, different portal submission.

Promotional / trade spend

KeHE

MCB (Minimum Case Buy at 8%), P&P promotional codes (IAA, S, P, IP, NP, PL). AAP enrollment exempts MCB.

UNFI

Own promotional fee structure. Different terminology for similar concepts. Trade spend appears differently on remittance.

Dispute window

KeHE: 180 days. UNFI: varies by promotional program.

Evidence

KeHE: deal sheet + PO + fee schedule + AAP enrollment status. UNFI: deal sheet + PO. Same agreements, different filing process.

Compliance / routing

KeHE

IBR fee schedule. Pallet specs (GMA 48x40), barcode compliance (95% scan rate), EDI deadlines (855 within 24h, 753 3 days before pickup).

UNFI

Own pallet, barcode, and scheduling requirements. Specs may vary by region and DC.

Dispute window

KeHE: 180 days. UNFI: varies.

Evidence

KeHE: EDI timestamps, C3 records, pallet photos. UNFI: own system records. Operational evidence is similar but portal requirements differ.

Damage / spoilage

KeHE

G prefix (per-event damage). CS (Customer Spoilage Allowance, fixed-rate deposit). Q (date-code failure). 48-hour UDR window applies.

UNFI

Own damage and spoilage codes. Different categorization between transit damage, concealed shortage, and date-code issues.

Dispute window

KeHE: 180 days. UNFI: varies by damage type.

Evidence

Both require photos, POD notations, temperature logs. Chain-of-custody documentation is critical at both. Same carrier records, different submission formats.

The real cost

The switching cost is not switching. It is running both.

Adding a second distributor does not double the work. It more than doubles it, because the systems share nothing and the team does not grow. Three specific costs compound.

The learning curve does not transfer

Mastering K-Solve does not teach you UNFI Direct. Knowing KeHE's 111 prefix codes does not help you classify a UNFI deduction. The systems, codes, portals, and processes are entirely separate. Every hour invested in one distributor's system builds no muscle memory for the other.

Evidence is the same, packaging is not

The BOL from your warehouse is the same document whether the shipment went to a KeHE DC or a UNFI DC. But the portal each distributor expects it uploaded to, the fields it needs tagged with, and the format it accepts are different. The same evidence gets packaged twice, in two different ways.

Deadlines compound, not average

KeHE gives you 180 days. UNFI timelines vary and are generally shorter for some categories. Both clocks tick simultaneously. A team focused on KeHE disputes this week may miss a UNFI deadline that expires Friday. Dual-distributor deadline management is a calendaring problem as much as an evidence problem.

Frequently asked

Does EasyOps support both KeHE and UNFI?+

EasyOps has deep KeHE expertise today: 111 deduction codes classified, a compliance guide, a K-Solve audit tool, and validated classification rules. UNFI support is actively in development. The Intelligent PO workspace is designed to normalize POs from both distributors, and deduction classification will adapt to each distributor's code system as coverage expands.

Can I use the same dispute evidence for both distributors?+

The underlying evidence, such as BOLs, PODs, and carrier records, is the same regardless of distributor. However, each distributor has different portal requirements for how that evidence is submitted, what fields are required, and what formats are accepted. The documents are the same; the packaging differs.

Which distributor is harder to manage deductions for?+

Neither is universally harder. KeHE has more deduction codes (111 prefix codes) and a strict 180-day dispute window through K-Solve only. UNFI has its own complexity with different timelines and processes. The real difficulty is managing both simultaneously with limited staff, because the systems do not share any common interface.

How do I track dispute deadlines across both distributors?+

Start with a single calendar view that shows deadlines for both distributors, coded by distributor. The Intelligent PO workspace is building toward this: surfacing dispute windows from both KeHE and UNFI against the same PO timeline, so nothing expires while the team is focused elsewhere.

What percentage of deductions are disputable at each distributor?+

At KeHE, EasyOps audit data shows approximately 88% of deduction dollars are contractual passthrough. The disputable portion is smaller than most brands expect. UNFI data is still being collected, but early patterns suggest a similar dynamic: the majority of deductions follow agreed terms. The exact split depends on your product categories, trade programs, and compliance track record.

What platform unifies deduction data from separate UNFI and KeHE portals?+

EasyOps normalizes purchase orders and deduction data from both KeHE and UNFI into a single workspace. Each distributor's deduction codes are classified using distributor-specific rules, but the investigation workflow, evidence collection, and dispute tracking are unified. The PO is the shared unit of work, not the distributor portal. KeHE coverage is production-ready today; UNFI classification is actively developing.

How do I transition from managing KeHE deductions to managing both KeHE and UNFI?+

Start by getting your KeHE deduction workflow running well — classification, evidence, disputes, prevention. The operational discipline transfers even though the systems do not. Then layer in UNFI: map its portal, learn its code system, and build a unified calendar for dispute deadlines across both distributors. EasyOps is designed to support this transition by normalizing both distributors into the same workspace.

Are UNFI deduction rates similar to KeHE's?+

Early patterns suggest a similar dynamic: the majority of deduction dollars at both distributors are contractual passthrough (trade spend, agreed fees, promotional allocations). The disputable portion is typically smaller than brands expect. Exact rates vary by category, compliance track record, and distribution volume. EasyOps will publish UNFI-specific deduction rate data as our validation reaches the depth of our KeHE analysis.

Can I use the same evidence for a dispute at both distributors?+

The underlying documents — BOLs, PODs, packing lists, ASNs, carrier tracking records — are the same regardless of which distributor the shipment went to. The evidence originates from your warehouse and carrier, not from the distributor. However, each distributor requires that evidence submitted through its own portal, with its own field names, attachment formats, and size limits. Same evidence, different packaging.
