Understanding K-Solve

What K-Solve is and why it matters

K-Solve is KeHE's supplier-facing deduction and dispute portal. It is the single source of truth for every deduction KeHE applies against your invoices, and the only accepted channel for filing disputes. Email dispute submissions are no longer accepted.

The portal contains transaction-level detail for every charge: the date it was applied, the amount, the prefix code that identifies the deduction type, the PO it was charged against, and the DC where the receiving event occurred. This data is exportable to Excel or CSV for offline analysis.

Quick Search: Covers approximately the last 90 days of deduction activity

Advanced Search: Reaches back up to 2 years of historical transaction detail

Dispute filing: The only accepted channel — submit directly through the portal

Export: Download the grid as Excel or CSV for offline review and audit

Key deadline

180

days to dispute

KeHE requires disputes filed through K-Solve within 180 days of the deduction date. A valid claim filed one day late is unrecoverable. This deadline drives everything in your review cadence.

Step by step

How to export your K-Solve data

The export is your starting point. Whether you're reviewing manually or uploading to the audit tool, you need a clean K-Solve export with the right date range.

01

Log in to KeHE Connect

Navigate to the KeHE supplier portal and sign in with your credentials. K-Solve is accessed through the main Connect platform.

02

Navigate to K-Solve deductions

From the main menu, find the K-Solve section. This is your deduction and dispute surface — the only channel KeHE accepts for dispute filing.

03

Select your date range

Quick Search covers roughly the last 90 days. Advanced Search reaches back up to 2 years. For a quarterly review, use Advanced Search and select the full quarter. For dispute-window awareness, export at least 180 days back.

04

Apply filters if needed

You can filter by DC, prefix code, amount range, or status. For a full audit, export unfiltered — let the classification tool or your review process handle the filtering.

05

Export to Excel or CSV

Click the export button to download the grid. Save the original file immediately and work from a copy. The original is your audit trail — never modify it directly.

06

Verify the export

Check that the row count matches what K-Solve displayed. Confirm the date range and total dollar amount. A mismatched export creates false conclusions downstream.

Column reference

What each column in the K-Solve export means

The K-Solve export contains several columns. The prefix code is the most important — it determines the deduction type and whether the charge may be disputable. Here's what each column tells you.

ColumnWhat it means
Transaction DateThe date the deduction was applied to your account. This starts the 180-day dispute clock. The further back the date, the more urgently you need to evaluate it.
AmountThe dollar value deducted. Negative values are charges against your invoice. Sort by this column descending to triage the largest deductions first.
Prefix CodeThe two-to-four character code that identifies the deduction type. KeHE uses 111 distinct prefix codes. This is the single most important field for classification — it tells you what kind of charge this is.
Reference / Check NumberThe remittance or payment reference linking the deduction to a specific settlement. Use this to cross-reference against your bank statement or accounts receivable ledger.
PO NumberThe retailer-issued purchase order this deduction was charged against. This is your anchor — every piece of dispute evidence must trace back to this PO.
DC / LocationThe distribution center where the receiving event occurred. Recurring deductions at a specific DC often indicate a systemic issue (routing, receiving practices, or label scanning) rather than a one-off error.
DescriptionFree-text description of the charge. Quality varies: some lines carry detailed explanations, others are terse codes. The prefix code is more reliable for classification than the description.

KeHE uses 111 distinct prefix codes. See the full prefix code library for every code explained in plain English with confidence levels.

Manual review

How to read the export manually

If you are reviewing K-Solve data without a tool, this is the process. A 400-line export typically takes 4-8 hours to review thoroughly. The steps are straightforward — the time cost is in the matching and evidence gathering.

1

Sort by amount (descending)

Start with the largest deductions. A $9,300 shortage matters more than a $12 EP processing fee. Your time is finite; prioritize by dollar impact.

2

Identify the prefix code on each line

The prefix code tells you the deduction type. Without knowing the code, you cannot classify the charge. Cross-reference against the 111-code library to understand what each code means.

3

Match each line to its PO

The PO number in the export must match a real purchase order in your system. An unmatched line needs investigation before it can be classified or disputed. Do not dispute a deduction you cannot tie to a PO.

4

Check the dispute window

Calculate days remaining from the Transaction Date. Anything approaching the 180-day deadline gets priority regardless of amount. A valid dispute filed one day late is unrecoverable.

5

Separate contractual from disputable

Most deduction dollars are contractual passthrough — charges that follow agreed terms. In one verified audit, 88% of deduction dollars were contractual. Identify these first so your dispute capacity goes to the lines that actually have a chance.

6

Assemble evidence for disputable items

For each line you intend to dispute, gather the BOL, POD, packing list, ASN, and any communication that speaks to the specific cause. Generic documents get rejected. The evidence must be PO-specific.

Time estimate

A manual review of a typical quarterly K-Solve export (200-400 lines) takes 4-8 hours for an experienced ops person. This includes identifying codes, matching POs, checking windows, and assembling evidence for the disputable lines. The same analysis takes about 5 minutes with the free audit tool.

Patterns to watch

Five patterns that signal recoverable money

Most deduction dollars are contractual. But inside the noise, certain patterns consistently indicate charges that should not have been taken or were taken incorrectly. Here are the five most common patterns to look for in your K-Solve export.

Double deductions (IA + KC on same PO)

KeHE takes an IA (Invoice Adjustment) at receiving and then a KC (post-receipt audit) on the same PO for the same shortage. The same charge appears twice under different codes. These have a historically high win rate because KeHE's own records show the duplicate.

Always check: does a prior IA exist for this PO before disputing a KC?

Recurring CN charges that spike

Weekly CN (Credit Note) pass-throughs are contractual by default — promotional costs, slotting fees, MCB charges. But when the weekly amount jumps 2x above the trailing 4-week average, or a CN appears for a DC you do not ship to, it may be a billing anomaly.

Flag CN lines that exceed the trailing average by 2x or appear on unexpected DCs.

Fill-rate fees on POs where you shipped 100%

The SLF (Service Level Fine) charges a 3% fee when fill rate drops below 95%. But the fill rate is measured at the DC receiving count, not at your warehouse. If your 3PL shipped 100% and KeHE's DC counted short, the evidence is in the BOL and pick confirmation.

Compare your pick confirmation and BOL against the KeHE receiving count.

OTD penalties where the DC rescheduled

On-time delivery penalties are calculated on a rolling quarter. But if the delivery was late because the DC rescheduled the appointment, the lateness is DC-initiated. The evidence is in the C3 Reservations appointment history showing who moved the date.

Pull the C3 appointment history before accepting any OTD penalty.

EP fees on AAP-enrolled accounts

The EP (Expedited Processing) fee is 8% of the invoice. But AAP (Automated Accounts Payable) enrolled suppliers are exempt from the MCB processing fee. If you are AAP-enrolled and seeing EP or MCB charges, verify your enrollment status and dispute.

Confirm AAP enrollment status. AAP-enrolled brands should not see MCB charges.

Frequently asked

K-Solve export questions

How often should I export and review K-Solve data?+

At minimum, monthly. Weekly is better for brands with high PO volume. The 180-day dispute window means a deduction from six months ago is about to expire. A monthly cadence catches everything with at least 150 days of window remaining. A quarterly cadence risks losing disputable items.

Can I export more than 90 days of K-Solve data?+

Yes. K-Solve Quick Search covers approximately the last 90 days, but Advanced Search reaches back up to 2 years of historical transaction detail. For a comprehensive audit, always use Advanced Search and export the full period you want to review.

What if I cannot find a deduction in K-Solve?+

Some deductions appear on the remittance but not yet in K-Solve. There can be a lag between when the charge posts to your account and when it appears in the K-Solve interface. If a remittance line has no K-Solve match, note the date and check again in 3-5 business days. If it still does not appear, escalate to your KeHE account representative.

Should I dispute every deduction I find?+

No. In one verified audit, 88% of deduction dollars ($140,914 of $151,671) were contractual passthrough — charges that follow agreed terms and should be verified, not disputed. Disputing contractual charges wastes your time, erodes your credibility with KeHE, and diverts capacity from genuinely recoverable items. Classify first, then dispute only the lines with supporting evidence.
