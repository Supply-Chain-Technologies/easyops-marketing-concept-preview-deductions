Direct answer

An audit connects a K-Solve line to its cause, evidence, and best next action.

KeHE uses 111 distinct deduction prefix codes. Each line in a K-Solve export represents a deduction against a specific invoice. The prefix tells you the type of charge. The investigation determines whether it is contractual, disputable, or still requires more information.

K-Solve column reference

Reading the K-Solve export: column by column.

Every K-Solve export contains these core columns. Knowing what each one tells you — and what it does not — is the first step in turning a spreadsheet of deductions into actionable intelligence.

Transaction date

When the deduction posted to your account.

What to check: Start the 180-day dispute clock from this date, not from when you discover the line.

Prefix code

The 2-3 character deduction type identifier. KeHE uses 111 distinct codes.

What to check: This is the single most important column. It determines the deduction type, the governing rule, and which evidence is relevant.

Reference number

Links the deduction to a specific PO, invoice, or internal KeHE reference.

What to check: Match this to your PO to anchor the evidence. Unmatched lines need investigation before they can be disputed.

Amount

The deduction dollar value. Negative values indicate money withheld from your payment.

What to check: Sort by absolute value to prioritize the highest-dollar lines first. A single large dispute often exceeds the sum of many small ones.

Description

KeHE's brief text label for the charge. Often abbreviated or generic.

What to check: Do not rely on the description alone — the prefix code is more precise. The description may say "adjustment" for several different deduction types.

DC / Location

Which distribution center processed the deduction.

What to check: Track deductions by DC to spot location-specific patterns — recurring shortages at one DC may indicate a receiving or labeling issue at that facility.

Period

The billing cycle the deduction falls within.

What to check: Use the period to reconcile against your remittance and to group deductions by billing cycle for trend analysis.

Export formats and practical limits

Format

K-Solve exports to Excel (.xlsx). The free audit tool also accepts CSV. Always preserve the original file before any manipulation — protect or duplicate it before you sort, filter, or add columns.

Size

A typical quarterly export for a mid-size CPG brand is 200-600 rows. Annual exports can reach 2,000+ rows across multiple billing cycles. The free audit tool handles exports up to 10MB.

History depth

K-Solve retains up to 2 years of data. Quick search covers ~90 days. For older data, use the advanced search function within the K-Solve portal and export the full date range.

The audit path

Eight controlled steps from export to operational learning.

1

Export

+

Start with the relevant K-Solve deduction data and preserve the original file. K-Solve retains up to two years of historical transaction detail. Export the grid as Excel, then protect the original before any manipulation.

2

Normalize

+

Connect each line to the PO, invoice, deduction date, amount, and reason code. The prefix code on each line identifies the deduction type. KeHE uses 111 distinct prefix codes, many without public documentation.

3

Classify

+

Separate contractual passthroughs from items that may warrant investigation or dispute. Not every deduction is recoverable. In a typical audit, the majority of deduction dollars are contractual.

4

Trace

+

Identify the governing requirement, transaction, shipment event, or document behind each deduction. This means matching the line to the specific PO, the applicable fee schedule version, and the operational event that triggered it.

5

Gather

+

Bring together BOLs, packing lists, photos, EDI records, appointment evidence, and messages. The evidence must be specific to the PO and the deduction date, not a general document.

6

Prioritize

+

Order the work by dollar value, evidence readiness, dispute-window deadlines, and likely operational impact. Disputes must be filed within 180 days of the deduction date.

7

Act

+

File through K-Solve (the only accepted channel) or route the next step internally. Include the dispute text, supporting evidence, and the specific PO and deduction references.

8

Learn

+

Carry the root cause back into the PO workflow to reduce repeat issues. A recurring shortages pattern, for example, may indicate a labeling, packing, or appointment-timing problem upstream.

What you will see

Common prefix codes and what they mean in practice.

KeHE uses 111 prefix codes. These six appear most often and illustrate how different codes require different investigative approaches. See the full reference for all 111 codes.

IAInvestigate

Invoice Adjustments

KeHE adjusted the invoice to match what was actually received or owed. Four sub-types: product short, missed allowance, supplier return, price discrepancy. Read the K-Solve backup sections independently.

SLFInvestigate

Service Level Fine

Fill rate fell below 95%, triggering a 3% fee on the shortage. Check whether your BOL and POD support the shipped quantity — DC count governs, but evidence can overturn it.

CNUsually contractual

Weekly Pass-Through

Retailer-initiated weekly charges routed through KeHE. Not a compliance chargeback. KeHE is the billing intermediary. Dispute route is the retailer, not K-Solve.

MCBInvestigate

MCB Processing Fee

The 8% processing fee on manufacturer chargebacks (promotional funding). AAP-enrolled suppliers are exempt. Verify enrollment status and fee math against the Fee Schedule.

CSUsually contractual

Customer Spoilage Allowance

Fixed-rate spoilage deposit taken on every PO. Verify the rate matches your Terms Form. Disputable only if rate is wrong, you are not enrolled, or there is a duplicate invoice.

GInvestigate

Damages or Spoils

Per-event damage charges for individual units. Requires photos, POD, temperature logs. Distinguish from CS (the proactive deposit) and Q (date-code failures). 48-hour UDR response window applies.

Common mistakes

Five mistakes that waste audit time.

Every one of these shows up in first-time K-Solve audits. Avoiding them concentrates effort where it actually recovers dollars.

Disputing contractual charges

In a verified audit, 88% of deduction dollars were contractual passthrough — charges that follow agreed terms. Disputing these wastes capacity and erodes credibility with KeHE. Classify first, then dispute only what has evidence.

Filing without PO-specific evidence

Generic BOLs or company-wide documents get rejected. KeHE expects evidence that ties to the specific PO number, shipment date, and deduction amount. A dispute without PO-specific proof is worse than no dispute.

Missing the 180-day window

K-Solve quick search only covers the last ~90 days. Advanced search reaches back 2 years, but the dispute window is 180 days from the deduction date. By the time most teams discover older deductions, the filing window has closed.

Treating every line independently

A recurring shortage pattern at a specific DC, or a CN charge that doubled overnight, only becomes visible when you look across POs. The highest-value audit findings come from patterns, not individual lines.

Ignoring the UDR 48-hour window

KeHE issues Unloading Discrepancy Reports at receiving. You have 48 hours to respond with counter-evidence. A missed UDR weakens every subsequent dispute on that PO because the DC count stands unchallenged.

Decision guide

Is this deduction disputable?

Three questions, in order. Stop at the first “no.”

1

Can you match it to a PO?

If the deduction cannot be connected to a specific purchase order, invoice, and shipment, it needs investigation — not a dispute.

No match → Investigate first

2

Is it contractual?

Check the prefix code against your Terms Form and Fee Schedule. If the charge follows an agreed term, verify the math — do not dispute.

Contractual → Verify, log, move on

3

Is the evidence ready?

Do you have the BOL, POD, or other documents specific to this PO and deduction date? A dispute without evidence is worse than no dispute.

Evidence ready → File through K-Solve

Frequently asked

Should every KeHE deduction be disputed?+

No. In the Sky & Sol audit, 88% of deduction dollars were contractual passthrough. Disputing those wastes time and credibility. A sound process distinguishes passthrough from genuinely disputable items.

What does K-Solve actually show?+

K-Solve shows that money was deducted and provides a reason code. It does not show the operational evidence (BOLs, photos, appointment records, carrier events) needed to dispute. That evidence lives in your 3PL, carrier, email, and EDI systems.

How long do I have to file a dispute?+

KeHE requires disputes to be filed within 180 days of the deduction date. Disputes are accepted only through the K-Solve portal. Email submissions are no longer accepted.

What evidence does KeHE expect?+

It depends on the deduction type. For shortages, a signed BOL or proof of delivery is decisive. For pricing disputes, the PO and signed deal sheet. For compliance chargebacks, timestamped EDI records and appointment documentation.

How does the free audit tool work?+

It separates contractual from disputable, ranks by value, and estimates recoverable dollars. It does not see your email, 3PL, or carrier evidence.

How does prevention connect to auditing?+

The same patterns that surface during an audit, such as recurring shortages at specific DCs or repeated timing violations, indicate operational issues that can be addressed upstream through routing, documentation, and fulfillment changes.

What columns does a K-Solve export contain?+

A K-Solve export typically includes: transaction date (when the deduction posted), prefix code (the 2-3 character deduction type identifier), reference number (links to the PO or invoice), amount (the deduction dollar value), description (KeHE's brief text), DC/location (which distribution center processed it), and period (the billing cycle). The prefix code is the most important column for classification — it determines the deduction type and which evidence is relevant.

How far back does K-Solve data go?+

K-Solve retains up to two years of historical transaction detail. However, the quick-search function only covers roughly the last 90 days. For older data, use the advanced search or export the full history. Note that the 180-day dispute window starts from the deduction date, not from when you discover it — so older deductions may already be past the filing deadline.

Quick reference

Your first 30 minutes with a K-Solve export.

Not every audit needs eight formal steps. If you have 30 minutes and a fresh K-Solve export, here is the fastest path to actionable intelligence.

Minute 0-5

Upload to the free audit tool

Drop the export at compliance-audit.tryeasyops.ai. The tool classifies every line in about 5 minutes — contractual vs. disputable, with estimated recoverable value. This gives you the full map before you touch a single row manually.

Minute 5-10

Review the disputable list

The audit tool ranks disputable items by dollar value and win probability. Read the top 5. These are the lines worth investigating first. Ignore contractual passthrough — it is 88% of the file in a typical audit.

Minute 10-20

Spot-check the top 3 lines

For each of the top 3 disputable lines: match the reference number to a PO, check whether you have a BOL or POD for that specific shipment, and confirm the 180-day window is still open. If all three are present, the dispute is ready to file.

Minute 20-30

Look for patterns

Sort the full export by DC, then by prefix code. Are the same codes appearing at the same location? Are fill-rate fees concentrated in one DC while another is clean? Patterns tell you where to fix upstream, not just which lines to dispute.
