Industry guide · Supplements & Vitamins

High-value units deserve deduction management that protects every dollar

Vitamin and supplement brands carry expensive, short-dated SKUs through KeHE and UNFI. A single short-date markdown or fill-rate fee erases the margin on dozens of units. This guide covers the deduction types that hit VMS brands hardest, the evidence each requires, and how to prevent the ones that are avoidable.

For CPG operations and finance teams managing supplement and vitamin deductions.

Direct answer

Same deduction types. Amplified margin impact.

VMS brands face the same deduction categories as grocery — service-level, compliance, and promotional — but the math hits harder. A grocery SKU with a $3 unit cost absorbs a 3% fill-rate fee as a rounding error. A supplement SKU at $45/unit turns that same percentage into a meaningful margin event.

On top of the universal deduction types, supplement brands face two category-specific risks: short-date markdowns from slower inventory turns, and labeling-compliance charges from the complexity of DSHEA-regulated supplement labels.

Five deduction types

The deductions that define the VMS P&L.

Various-$742.00

Short-date markdown

Trigger: Product received inside the distributor's minimum-remaining-shelf-life window. Slower-moving VMS SKUs are disproportionately exposed.

Disputable? Only if the receiving window rule was not actually breached — check the expiration date against the specific distributor threshold

Evidence: Lot-level expiration records, PO receiving date, distributor shelf-life policy

SLF-$611.25

Fill-rate fee

Trigger: Service level falls below 95% for the quarter. KeHE charges 3% of the shortage amount. Same trigger as grocery, but each unit's dollar value amplifies the fee.

Disputable? Often — when DC miscount caused the shortfall, not supplier short-ship

Evidence: Signed BOL, POD, pick confirmation, warehouse count records

Various-$300.00

Label / spec compliance

Trigger: Case pack or label does not match the approved item setup. DSHEA structure-function claims, supplement facts panel, or case dimensions differ from what KeHE has on file.

Disputable? Yes — when the label matches the approved item setup and the scan was incorrect

Evidence: Approved item-setup record, label proof, case dimension verification

MCB-$220.80

EP processing fee

Trigger: 8% of invoice for the Expedited Program. AAP-enrolled suppliers are exempt.

Disputable? Yes — if AAP enrollment status is current

Evidence: AAP enrollment confirmation, Fee Schedule, invoice

Various-$400–$1,200

Temperature non-compliance

Trigger: Product arrives outside the required temperature band. Refrigerated supplements must be 34-40°F. Some probiotic and enzyme SKUs have stricter requirements.

Disputable? Only with continuous temperature logging from pickup through delivery

Evidence: Temperature data logger records, carrier handoff documentation, pre-load temperature photo

Why VMS is different

Three dynamics that amplify deduction impact for supplement brands.

Slower turns = short-date risk

Vitamins and supplements turn 2-5x slower than grocery. A 12-month shelf life product that sits in your warehouse for 4 months arrives at the DC with 8 months remaining — and if the distributor requires 9 months minimum, it triggers a markdown. Grocery products with 60-day shelf life and weekly turns rarely face this math.

Complex labeling = compliance risk

Supplement labels carry DSHEA structure-function claims, supplement facts panels, allergen warnings, and lot-level traceability requirements. A single spec mismatch between your label proof and the distributor's item setup becomes a compliance deduction. Grocery labels are simpler — fewer regulatory dimensions to drift out of alignment.

Higher unit cost = each deduction hurts more

A grocery SKU retails for $3-8 per unit. A supplement SKU retails for $15-60+. When a fill-rate fee is 3% of the shortage amount, the dollar impact per case is 3-8x higher for supplements. A $611 fill-rate fee on a supplement PO might equal $180 on the same case count of a grocery product.

Prevention: shelf-life management

Four controls that prevent the most expensive VMS deduction.

Short-date markdowns are the most category-specific and most preventable deduction for supplement brands. These four controls address the root cause.

1

Implement FEFO inventory management

First-Expiry, First-Out ensures the oldest lot ships first. This is the single most effective control against short-date deductions. If your WMS supports FEFO at the lot level, enable it for every VMS SKU.

2

Know each distributor's minimum-remaining-shelf-life rule

KeHE and UNFI each have their own threshold — typically 60-75% of total shelf life remaining at receipt. A product with 12-month shelf life needs to arrive with 8-9 months remaining. Know the exact number for each distributor and each product category.

3

Track lots from production through shipment

Every PO should carry lot-level detail in the ASN (EDI 856). When a short-date deduction lands, you need to trace it back to the specific lot, its production date, and when it entered your warehouse. Without lot tracking, you cannot determine whether the issue was production timing, warehouse dwell, or carrier delay.

4

Run pre-shipment expiration checks

Before the pick ticket prints, verify that the lots being allocated will arrive inside the distributor's minimum-remaining-shelf-life window. This is a calculation: production date + total shelf life - transit time - distributor threshold = last-ship date. If today is past the last-ship date, route the lot elsewhere.

Frequently asked

Supplement deduction questions.

Why do supplement brands get more short-date deductions than grocery?+

Slower inventory turns. A supplement SKU with 12-month shelf life that sits in the warehouse for 4 months arrives at the DC with only 8 months remaining. If the distributor requires 9+ months, it triggers a markdown. Grocery products turn fast enough that shelf-life deductions are rare.

Are labeling-compliance deductions disputable?+

Sometimes. If the label or case pack matches the approved item setup and the distributor scanned it incorrectly, the charge is disputable with the item-setup record as evidence. If the label genuinely differs from spec (a common issue after label revisions), it is a valid charge to fix upstream. EasyOps classifies which is which.

How does EasyOps prioritize supplement deductions?+

By dollar exposure, not count. A supplement brand with $60/unit SKUs needs to dispute the high-value fill-rate fees first, not the high-volume small charges. EasyOps ranks every disputable deduction by recoverable value so effort goes where margin actually moves.

Can I prevent temperature compliance deductions?+

Yes — with continuous temperature logging. Install a temperature data logger in every pallet before the carrier takes possession. If the product arrives outside the required band, the logger shows whether the temperature breach happened at your facility, during transit, or at the DC. Without the logger, you have no counter-evidence.

How do supplement deductions differ between KeHE and UNFI?+

Both distributors charge for short-date, fill-rate, and compliance failures. The thresholds, code systems, and dispute processes differ. KeHE uses 111 prefix codes and K-Solve for disputes (180-day window). UNFI uses its own code system and UNFI Direct portal with different timelines. The underlying evidence (BOL, POD, lot records) is the same — the packaging differs.

Evidence boundary: This guide is an operational reference for VMS brands. Shelf-life thresholds and compliance requirements vary by distributor and product category. Always verify current terms directly with each distributor.
