UNFI deduction management: what CPG brands need to know

For CPG brands selling through UNFI — understanding deduction types, the UNFI Direct portal, dispute timelines, and how to classify what's recoverable.

UNFI applies deductions against supplier invoice payments for shortages, compliance violations, promotional charges, and service-level failures — similar to KeHE but with its own portal, its own code system, and its own dispute timelines.

Direct answer

UNFI deductions work like KeHE's — different portal, different codes, same operational pain.

Every CPG brand selling through UNFI sees money withheld from invoice payments: shortage claims, compliance fees, promotional chargebacks, and service-level penalties. The deduction types are functionally similar to KeHE, but every operational detail — the portal interface, the code system, the dispute timeline, the evidence format — is different. Managing both distributors simultaneously is where the real complexity lives.

Common UNFI deduction types

Six deduction types that appear on most UNFI remittances.

Each type has its own cause, evidence requirements, and dispute path. Not every deduction is recoverable — the first step is always classification.

Shortage claims

Typical cause

UNFI receives fewer cases than the ASN or invoice states.

Disputable?

Often disputable

Evidence needed

BOL, signed POD, packing list, carrier count records

Compliance fees

Typical cause

Routing violations, appointment misses, labeling non-compliance, or pallet specification failures.

Disputable?

Depends on cause

Evidence needed

Appointment confirmation, routing compliance records, label specs, pallet documentation

Promotional / trade spend

Typical cause

Agreed promotional allowances, slotting fees, and placement charges deducted from remittance.

Disputable?

Usually contractual

Evidence needed

Signed promotional agreement, deal sheet, PO terms

Service level penalties

Typical cause

Fill rate below threshold or repeated delivery failures within a measurement period.

Disputable?

Potentially disputable

Evidence needed

Warehouse pick confirmation, ASN data, carrier delivery records

Freight charges

Typical cause

Inbound freight, fuel surcharges, or logistics fees applied by UNFI.

Disputable?

Verify rate

Evidence needed

Freight agreement, rate confirmation, BOL weight and dimensions

Administrative fees

Typical cause

Processing fees, EDI non-compliance charges, or new-item setup fees.

Disputable?

Usually contractual

Evidence needed

Supplier agreement, EDI transmission records, item setup documentation

The portal

UNFI Direct: your supplier portal for deduction data and disputes.

UNFI Direct is UNFI's supplier-facing portal for account management, deduction reporting, and dispute submission. It is a completely separate system from KeHE's K-Solve — different URL, different credentials, different navigation, different field names.

Deduction reporting

View deductions by date, type, and status. Export data for analysis. UNFI uses its own code taxonomy, separate from KeHE's 111 prefix codes.

Dispute submission

File disputes through the portal with attached evidence. Submission requirements and accepted formats differ from K-Solve.

Account management

View invoices, remittances, and account status. Track dispute resolution and payment details.

Key difference

Two portals. Zero shared interface.

If your brand sells through both KeHE and UNFI, your ops team manages deductions across two completely separate systems. Different logins, different code systems, different dispute deadlines. The same BOL may need to be packaged differently for each submission.

KeHE vs. UNFI

Same deduction categories. Different operational reality.

Both distributors deduct for shortages, compliance, promotions, and service failures. The operational details — portals, codes, timelines, evidence formats — are different at every step.

Dispute portal

UNFI

UNFI Direct: supplier-facing portal with dispute submission, deduction reporting, and account management.

KeHE

K-Solve: KeHE's supplier-facing deduction and dispute portal. Shows deduction data and is the only accepted dispute channel.

Code system

UNFI

UNFI uses its own deduction code taxonomy. Different naming conventions and groupings from KeHE.

KeHE

KeHE uses 111 distinct prefix codes. EasyOps has validated classification rules for 48 at 100% accuracy.

Dispute window

UNFI

Varies by deduction type. Timelines are generally shorter for some categories. Check current UNFI terms.

KeHE

180 days from the deduction date. Strict cutoff. Portal-only submission through K-Solve.

Evidence format

UNFI

Portal submissions through UNFI Direct. Attachment formats and size limits differ from KeHE.

KeHE

Portal-only through K-Solve. Expects PO-specific evidence: BOLs, PODs, signed deal sheets, EDI records.

Remittance format

UNFI

UNFI remittance uses its own field structure and naming conventions.

KeHE

KeHE remittance uses prefix codes that map to the 111-code system. K-Solve exports to Excel.

The workflow

Classify. Dispute. Prevent. The same framework applies.

Regardless of distributor, effective deduction management follows the same three steps. The rules and codes are distributor-specific; the operating discipline is universal.

1

Classify every deduction

Upload your deduction data. Separate contractual passthroughs from charges that may be disputable. Do not treat every deduction as recoverable — the majority are typically contractual.

With EasyOps

EasyOps classifies each line as contractual passthrough or disputable using validated, up-to-date rules specific to each distributor.

2

Dispute with evidence

For each disputable deduction, assemble evidence specific to the PO and deduction date: BOL, POD, appointment records, and the rule citation. File through the correct portal before the deadline.

With EasyOps

EasyOps assembles the evidence packet automatically from connected systems and drafts the dispute matched to the deduction type.

3

Prevent the next one

Track recurring causes by DC, deduction type, and time period. Route patterns into routing, documentation, and fulfillment changes. Recovery gets the dollar back; prevention keeps it from leaving.

With EasyOps

EasyOps surfaces root cause patterns and checks compliance rules before shipment so fewer deductions happen in the first place.

Frequently asked

Does EasyOps support UNFI deductions?+

EasyOps is actively developing UNFI deduction classification. KeHE coverage is deepest today, with validated rules for 48 of 111 prefix codes at 100% accuracy. UNFI coverage is expanding — the Intelligent PO workspace is designed to normalize POs from both distributors, and deduction classification adapts to each distributor's code system as coverage grows.

How do UNFI deductions differ from KeHE?+

UNFI and KeHE use different portals (UNFI Direct vs. K-Solve), different code systems, different dispute timelines, and different evidence format requirements. The underlying deduction types are similar — shortages, compliance fees, promotional charges, service-level penalties — but the operational details differ at every step. See our full comparison guide for details.

What is the UNFI dispute deadline?+

UNFI dispute deadlines vary by deduction type and category. Some categories have shorter windows than KeHE's 180-day cutoff. Always verify current deadlines directly with UNFI, as terms are updated periodically.

Can I manage both KeHE and UNFI deductions in one system?+

Yes. The Intelligent PO workspace handles orders from both distributors in the same interface. The PO is the unit of work, not the distributor portal. Classification rules are specific to each distributor's code system, but the investigation and dispute workflow is the same.

What evidence does UNFI need for a dispute?+

UNFI expects PO-specific evidence submitted through UNFI Direct. Common evidence includes BOLs, PODs, packing lists, appointment records, and signed agreements. Evidence requirements vary by deduction type. The format and attachment specifications differ from KeHE — the same document may need to be packaged differently for each distributor.
