Point-solution landscape

Competitors you will encounter.

These companies occupy different positions in the KeHE deduction management space. This comparison is factual, dated September 2026, and based on publicly available information. Always verify current capabilities directly.

OverDeduct

Free deduction audit tool + self-serve software + contingency recovery service. Targets emerging CPG brands.

Strength

Free audit entry point; comparison content ranks well in search; covers both KeHE and UNFI.

Limitation

Starts at the deduction, not the PO. No upstream prevention, no systems integration, no evidence assembly from EDI/WMS/email.

Confido

Deduction management platform with OCR intake and categorization. Originally focused on Costco/Target deductions.

Strength

Clean UI; good at categorizing deductions from scanned backup PDFs.

Limitation

KeHE-specific depth is limited. No prefix-code classification at the level of 111 codes. No PO-level operational context.

iNymbus

Automated deduction management and dispute filing. Enterprise-oriented, covers multiple retailers.

Strength

Scale automation for high-volume dispute filing. Good content marketing for KeHE K-Solve how-to queries.

Limitation

Enterprise pricing and implementation. Not designed for emerging/scaling CPG brands ($500K-$5M revenue). Prevention is not the focus.

SPS Commerce

EDI and supply chain platform with a Revenue Recovery add-on for deduction management.

Strength

EDI infrastructure already in place for many brands. Revenue Recovery product handles dispute filing.

Limitation

Deduction management is an add-on, not the core product. KeHE-specific compliance depth is limited.

Glimpse

End-to-end deduction management combining software and managed service.

Strength

Combines technology with human analysts. Handles the work for the brand.

Limitation

Managed-service model means the brand builds no internal capability. Revenue depends on deductions continuing — same incentive misalignment as traditional recovery services.

EasyOps

The Intelligent PO™ — a workspace that connects the operational story around each purchase order. Starts upstream (prevention), covers the middle (operations), and handles the tail (recovery).

Differentiator

PO-centric (not deduction-centric). 111 KeHE codes classified. Connects EDI, WMS, email, and portals. Prevention + recovery in one workflow.

Honest limitation

Deep KeHE expertise; UNFI coverage still developing. Best for brands at $500K-$5M revenue scaling through KeHE.

Evaluation framework

Questions to ask before you choose.

These questions apply regardless of whether you're evaluating software, a service, an internal hire, or a point solution. The answers will reveal which model fits your operating reality.

Data and evidence

  • What data sources does the solution need access to? EDI, ERP, WMS, email, portals?
  • Can it connect to the systems you actually use, or does it require manual uploads?
  • Does it collect evidence upstream (before the deduction) or only after?

Workflow and integration

  • Does it replace your existing tools or connect between them?
  • How does it handle the handoff between operations, finance, and sales roles?
  • Can your 3PL, broker, or carrier see the context they need?

Pricing and incentive alignment

  • Is the pricing based on subscription, share of recovery, or per-transaction?
  • Does the provider benefit when your deductions decrease — or only when they stay high?
  • What happens to cost as your wholesale volume grows?

Distributor specificity

  • How many of KeHE's 111 prefix codes does the tool classify? At what accuracy?
  • Does the solution track distributor-specific fee schedules and compliance rules?
  • Can it handle both KeHE and UNFI, or does each require a separate tool?

Prevention vs. recovery

  • Does the solution catch compliance violations before the truck moves?
  • Can it flag billing anomalies the day they post, or only during periodic audits?
  • Does it feed deduction root causes back into operational improvements?

Auditability and ownership

  • Can you see the rules behind every classification decision?
  • Do you own your data, or does it stay in the provider's system?
  • What happens to your evidence and dispute history if you leave the platform?

The strongest answer may be a combination.

Software can provide shared context and repeatable workflow. People still make decisions, manage relationships, and handle exceptions. A service may add specialized execution for a defined scope. A point solution may cover a specific gap. The evaluation should begin with which jobs are recurring, which require judgment, and which are currently being abandoned.
