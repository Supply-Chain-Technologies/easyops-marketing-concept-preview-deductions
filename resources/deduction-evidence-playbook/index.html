Deduction evidence & dispute playbook

What evidence each retailer and distributor expects, organized by deduction type. Filing deadlines, common rejection reasons, and how to build a packet that wins.

Direct answer

The evidence depends on the cause, not the dollar amount.

A $500 shortage dispute and a $50,000 shortage dispute need the same documents: a signed BOL, proof of delivery, and packing list specific to the PO. A pricing dispute needs the PO, the invoice, and the governing agreement. Filing with incomplete evidence wastes the dispute and often cannot be resubmitted.

This playbook organizes the evidence requirements by deduction type and distributor, so your team knows exactly what to gather before filing.

KeHE · Deepest coverage

KeHE evidence requirements by deduction type

KeHE requires all disputes to be filed through K-Solve within 180 days. Email submissions are no longer accepted. Evidence must be PO-specific — generic company documents are rejected.

1

Shortage disputes (IA, KC)

Qty received < qty invoiced. The most common disputable deduction type. Watch for the double-deduction pattern: if KeHE takes an IA at receiving and then a KC from a post-receipt audit on the same PO, that is a double charge with a historically high win rate.

Required evidence

Signed BOL (bill of lading)

Proves qty loaded at origin. Must match the PO and shipment, not a generic document.

Proof of delivery (POD)

DC-signed receipt confirming delivered qty. Decisive for shortages caused by DC miscounts.

Packing list / pick confirmation

Warehouse record of what was picked, packed, and staged for the carrier.

ASN (EDI 856)

Advance ship notice transmitted before delivery. Timestamps and lot detail corroborate the BOL.

UDR response (within 48 hours)

KeHE's Unit Discrepancy Report must be acknowledged within 48 hours of receiving. A missed UDR weakens any subsequent dispute.

2

Compliance & routing (FF, IBR)

Late delivery, missed appointment, no-show, or routing non-compliance. Distinguish appointment-class fees (both delivery methods) from lumping-class fees (Supplier Delivered only).

Required evidence

C3 Reservations appointment history

Shows scheduled vs actual delivery times. Proves whether a no-show or reschedule was supplier- or DC-initiated.

Carrier tracking events

Pickup scan, in-transit milestones, and delivery confirmation. Proves the shipment was on time from the supplier's side.

EDI transmission timestamps

Acknowledgement records for 855, 753, and 856. The 855 must be sent within 24 hours of the PO — the deadline suppliers most often discover after missing it.

Communication trail

Emails or portal messages showing who moved the appointment or changed delivery requirements.

3

Pricing & allowances (IA, MCB)

Invoice price vs agreed price, promotional allowance discrepancies, and MCB processing fee disputes. AAP-enrolled suppliers are exempt from the MCB 8% fee.

Required evidence

Purchase order (EDI 850)

The original PO terms — price, qty, item detail. The anchor for any pricing dispute.

Invoice (EDI 810)

What was billed. Must match the PO and delivery.

Signed deal sheet or promotion agreement

The governing agreement for promotional allowances, slotting fees, or pricing adjustments.

AAP enrollment confirmation

For MCB disputes — AAP-enrolled suppliers are exempt from the MCB 8% processing fee. Verify enrollment status before disputing.

Prior deductions under the same term

If the same allowance was already taken on a prior PO, document it to flag potential double charges.

4

Damage & spoilage (G, CS, Q)

Product damaged in transit, spoilage allowances, and date-code failures. Distinguish G (verifiable damage) from CS (fixed-rate allowance) and Q (date-code — usually valid, fix upstream).

Required evidence

Photos at handoff

Condition of product when transferred to the carrier. Timestamps and context matter.

POD notations

Any receiving notes about visible damage, temperature deviation, or short-coded product.

Temperature logs

For refrigerated and frozen: continuous temperature records from pickup through delivery.

Carrier condition report

The carrier's own record of product condition at pickup. Distinguishes supplier-side vs transit damage.

5

Late delivery (OTD penalties)

On-time delivery fell below the quarterly threshold (commonly 85%). Often disputable when the appointment was rescheduled by the distributor or the delivery was signed within the original window.

Required evidence

Appointment records (C3 Reservations)

Original booking, any reschedules, and who initiated changes.

Carrier tracking events

Proves the shipment departed on time from the supplier. Differentiates supplier delay from carrier delay.

Communication trail

Emails or calls showing DC-initiated changes, weather events, or other force-majeure circumstances.

BOL with timestamps

Pickup and delivery times that prove the shipment was within the agreed window.

UNFI · Expanding coverage

UNFI evidence requirements

UNFI disputes are filed through UNFI Direct — a separate portal with its own interface, field requirements, and submission process. The underlying evidence is the same, but the packaging differs. UNFI dispute timelines vary by deduction category and may be shorter than KeHE's 180-day window.

Coverage boundary: UNFI dispute procedures are documented here at a general level based on publicly available supplier documentation and EasyOps operational research. KeHE evidence coverage is deeper and validated against production data.

Why disputes fail

Common rejection reasons

Most rejected disputes fail for one of five reasons. Knowing them before filing prevents wasted effort and preserves credibility with the distributor.

1

Evidence not PO-specific

The most common rejection. A generic BOL or company-wide document does not prove what happened on a specific order. Every piece of evidence must reference the PO number, shipment date, and relevant quantities.

2

Filed outside the dispute window

KeHE enforces a strict 180-day window through K-Solve. No exceptions. UNFI timelines vary by category but are generally shorter for some deduction types. A valid claim filed one day late is unrecoverable.

3

Missing required document

A shortage dispute without a BOL. A compliance dispute without the appointment record. A pricing dispute without the deal sheet. The packet must be complete for the deduction type, not just fast.

4

Disputing a contractual charge

In one audit, 88% of deduction dollars were contractual passthrough — charges that follow agreed terms. Disputing these wastes credibility and capacity. Classify first, then dispute.

5

Insufficient detail in the submission

A dispute that says "we disagree" without specifics. Strong disputes cite the rule, the PO, and the evidence that contradicts the charge. Weak disputes get rejected and cannot be resubmitted.

Timeline

Dispute timeline quick reference

From the day a deduction posts through the 180-day KeHE window close. Faster filing with complete evidence correlates with higher win rates.

Day 0

Deduction posts

The charge appears on your remittance or in K-Solve / UNFI Direct. Preserve the original line, amount, date, code, and reference fields.

Day 1

Classify and match to PO

Determine whether the charge is contractual passthrough or potentially disputable. Match it to the purchase order, shipment, and receiving event.

Day 2–5

Assemble evidence packet

Gather the specific documents for this deduction type and this PO. Every document must be PO-specific and date-relevant.

Day 5–10

File the dispute

Submit through K-Solve (KeHE) or UNFI Direct (UNFI) with the complete evidence packet. Confirm submission and save the reference number.

Day 10–30

Track to resolution

Standard response SLA is approximately 3 weeks for KeHE. Monitor status and follow up on unresolved disputes. An unanswered dispute is an unfinished dispute.

Day 180

KeHE window closes

After 180 days, the dispute cannot be filed. UNFI windows vary by category. Calendar deadlines for both distributors and prioritize by window proximity.

Frequently asked

What evidence does KeHE require for a shortage dispute?+

A signed BOL, proof of delivery (POD), packing list or pick confirmation, and the ASN (EDI 856). If KeHE issued a UDR (Unit Discrepancy Report), your response within 48 hours is also required. The evidence must be specific to the PO and shipment date — generic company documents are rejected. For double-deductions (IA followed by KC on the same PO), the prior IA record is the strongest evidence and carries a historically high win rate.

Can I use the same evidence for KeHE and UNFI?+

The underlying documents — BOLs, PODs, photos, carrier records — are the same regardless of distributor, because they come from your warehouse and carrier. However, each distributor has different portal requirements for how that evidence is submitted, what fields are required, and what formats are accepted. The documents are the same; the packaging differs.

What happens if I miss the dispute window?+

The deduction becomes unrecoverable. KeHE enforces a strict 180-day cutoff through K-Solve with no exceptions. UNFI timelines vary by deduction category and may be shorter. This is why dispute deadline calendaring — tracking windows across both distributors — is one of the highest-value operational improvements a CPG team can make.

Does EasyOps generate dispute letters?+

Yes. EasyOps drafts dispute submissions matched to the specific deduction type, with the evidence packet the distributor expects already attached. For KeHE, disputes file through K-Solve. For UNFI, the system prepares the dispute text and evidence for portal submission. The evidence is assembled during fulfillment — not after the deduction lands — so the packet is already built when the charge appears.
