Who this is for

CPG ops and finance teams evaluating their options.

If you are a CPG brand managing deductions from KeHE, UNFI, Walmart, Target, Costco, or other retailers and distributors, you have options. The market includes enterprise platforms, managed services, SaaS tools, and free point solutions. This guide maps the landscape so you can evaluate based on your specific volume, team size, distributor mix, and budget.

Disclosure:EasyOps is one of the tools compared on this page. We are the publisher. We have described every competitor's strengths and limitations based on publicly available information. If you find an inaccuracy, email hello@tryeasyops.com.

Market overview

Three tiers of deduction management tools.

The CPG deduction management market is not one category. It spans enterprise automation platforms, managed services, and SaaS tools — each with different economics, implementation timelines, and target customers.

Enterprise automation

High-volume platforms designed for large companies processing thousands of deductions monthly. Typically require weeks-to-months implementation.

iNymbus, HighRadius, SPS Commerce

Managed service

Outsourced providers that combine software with human analysts. They manage the deduction workflow for you, typically taking a share of recovered dollars.

Glimpse, Smyyth

Platform / point tools

Software tools that give your team direct visibility, classification, and dispute capabilities. Platform fee, not percentage of recovered.

EasyOps, OverDeduct, Confido

Vendor profiles

Six competitors, examined honestly.

Each profile describes what the vendor does, who they serve best, their strongest advantage, and where they fall short — based on publicly available information.

Glimpse

Managed service

End-to-end deduction management combining software with human analysts who manage disputes on your behalf.

Best for: CPG brands that want someone else to handle deduction recovery without building internal capability.

Strongest advantage

11 published case studies with specific dollar amounts ($142K–$2M+ recovered). Combines technology with dedicated analyst teams. Active promotions including a "$10K guarantee" for larger brands and "3 months free" for emerging brands.

Where they fall short

Managed-service model means the brand builds no internal deduction management capability. Revenue depends on deductions continuing — if deductions decrease, Glimpse earns less, creating the same incentive misalignment as traditional recovery services. Pricing is not publicly disclosed but is believed to be incentive-aligned (percentage of recovered).

Pricing: Not publicly disclosed. Believed to be incentive-aligned (share of recovered).

OverDeduct

Point tool

Free deduction audit tool with self-serve software and a contingency recovery service for brands that want help filing.

Best for: Emerging CPG brands that want a free starting point for understanding their deductions before committing to a tool or service.

Strongest advantage

Free audit entry point removes all friction from first interaction. Comparison content ranks well in search. Covers both KeHE and UNFI deductions.

Where they fall short

Starts at the deduction, not the purchase order. No upstream prevention, no systems integration (EDI, WMS, email), and no evidence assembly from operational data. The free audit identifies deductions but does not connect them to operational context.

Pricing: Free audit tier. Self-serve software and contingency recovery pricing not publicly disclosed.

iNymbus

Enterprise automation

Automated deduction management and dispute filing platform designed for high-volume enterprise CPG and retail operations.

Best for: Large CPG companies ($100M+ revenue) processing thousands of deductions monthly across many retail partners.

Strongest advantage

Scale automation for high-volume dispute filing. Strong content marketing for KeHE K-Solve how-to queries. Covers multiple retailers beyond KeHE and UNFI. Cloud-based robotic process automation approach.

Where they fall short

Enterprise pricing and implementation timeline. Not designed for emerging or scaling CPG brands ($500K–$30M revenue). Prevention and pre-shipment compliance checking are not the focus — the platform starts at the deduction, not the PO.

Pricing: Enterprise pricing. Not publicly disclosed. Implementation timeline typically weeks to months.

HighRadius

Enterprise automation

AI-powered Order-to-Cash platform with a Deduction Management module that sits alongside AR automation, credit, and collections.

Best for: Enterprise finance teams already using or evaluating HighRadius for broader order-to-cash automation.

Strongest advantage

Part of a comprehensive O2C suite — deductions connect to AR, credit, and collections in one platform. Strong AI and automation capabilities. Well-funded ($475M+ raised) with a large customer base.

Where they fall short

Deduction management is one module in a larger suite, not the core focus. KeHE and UNFI-specific depth (prefix codes, compliance rules, routing guides) is limited compared to CPG-specialized tools. Enterprise pricing and implementation.

Pricing: Enterprise pricing. Not publicly disclosed.

Confido

Point tool

Deduction management platform with OCR intake and categorization, originally focused on Costco and Target deductions.

Best for: CPG brands dealing primarily with retailer-direct deductions (Costco, Target, Walmart) who need help categorizing backup PDFs.

Strongest advantage

Clean UI. Good at categorizing deductions from scanned backup PDFs using OCR. Breadth across retail channels.

Where they fall short

KeHE-specific depth is limited — no prefix-code classification at the level of 111 individual codes. No PO-level operational context connecting deductions to purchase orders, shipments, and compliance evidence. Limited prevention capability.

Pricing: SaaS pricing. Not publicly disclosed.

SPS Commerce

Enterprise automation

EDI and supply chain platform with a Revenue Recovery add-on for automated deduction management and dispute filing.

Best for: Brands already using SPS Commerce for EDI who want to add deduction management without onboarding a new vendor.

Strongest advantage

EDI infrastructure already in place for many CPG brands. Revenue Recovery product handles dispute filing. Large network of retailer connections. Publicly traded, stable vendor.

Where they fall short

Deduction management is an add-on, not the core product. KeHE-specific compliance depth (routing rules, fee schedules, prefix codes) is limited. Prevention and pre-shipment compliance checking are not the focus of the Revenue Recovery module.

Pricing: Add-on pricing to existing SPS Commerce subscription. Not publicly disclosed.

Before you decide

Five questions to ask any vendor.

These questions apply regardless of which tool or service you are evaluating. The answers will tell you more than any feature comparison.

01

What is your classification accuracy, and how do you validate it?

Any vendor that cannot cite a specific number with a defined validation methodology is guessing. Ask for the denominator: "100% accuracy on what? 10 codes or 100?" A vendor that says "AI-powered" without a measurable accuracy figure is describing the method, not the result.

02

Do you file disputes, or just identify them?

Identification without filing creates a to-do list, not a recovery. Ask whether the tool submits directly to the distributor portal (K-Solve, UNFI Direct) or produces a report you file manually. A report is useful; an auto-filed dispute with evidence attached is a recovery.

03

What happens after recovery — does the root cause get fixed?

Recovery gets the dollar back. Prevention keeps it from leaving. Ask whether the tool surfaces recurring patterns (by DC, by cause, by time period) that feed upstream operational changes. If the answer is only about recovery, the tool is downstream-only.

04

How are you priced — by recovered dollar, by PO, or flat fee?

Percentage-of-recovered creates a structural incentive misalignment: the provider profits from your deductions continuing. A platform fee aligns the provider's interest with reducing your deductions over time. Ask the vendor to explain what happens to their revenue if your deductions drop 50%.

05

Can I see my own data during the evaluation?

The strongest signal is whether the vendor will run your actual deduction data during the demo, not just show a canned environment. Ask to upload a real K-Solve or UNFI export and see the results before signing anything. If they say no, ask why.

Frequently asked

Common questions about this comparison.

Is this comparison biased?+

Yes — this page is published by EasyOps, and we are one of the tools compared. We have tried to describe every competitor's strengths and limitations factually and verifiably. If you find an inaccuracy, email hello@tryeasyops.com and we will correct it. We recommend verifying every claim on this page with the vendor directly.

How often is this comparison updated?+

We review this page quarterly and when the competitive landscape changes materially (new product launches, pricing changes, acquisitions). The "Last reviewed" date at the top reflects when the content was last verified.

Can I use multiple tools together?+

Yes. Many CPG brands use a combination: software for classification and ongoing workflow, a recovery service for above-threshold disputes they don't have time to file, and an internal person for judgment calls and relationship management. The question is which jobs are recurring (automate), which require judgment (staff), and which are currently being abandoned (tool or service).

What if I'm too small for enterprise tools?+

Enterprise tools like iNymbus and HighRadius are designed for high-volume operations — typically $100M+ revenue with thousands of deductions per month. If you are a $500K–$30M CPG brand, the implementation cost and timeline of enterprise tools may exceed the value they deliver. Look at tools designed for your stage: EasyOps, OverDeduct, or a focused recovery service.

How does pricing compare across these tools?+

Pricing models vary significantly. Recovery services typically charge 20–35% of recovered dollars. Enterprise platforms charge license fees that can run $50K–$200K+ annually. Point tools and platforms range from free tiers to $500–$5,000/month. EasyOps uses a platform fee model — not a percentage of recovered dollars. We recommend asking each vendor for a total-cost-of-ownership estimate including implementation, training, and ongoing fees.
