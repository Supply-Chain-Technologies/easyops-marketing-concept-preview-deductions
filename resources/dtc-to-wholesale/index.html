Cornerstone guide · Wholesale readiness

Wholesale is an operating model, not just a bigger order.

A DTC brand is ready for wholesale when it can receive a retailer-issued PO, meet the distributor’s rules, coordinate fulfillment, keep the evidence, own the deductions, and carry the cash cycle. This checklist covers all eight domains.

AuthorJordan Harper, CEO, EasyOps

Last reviewedAugust 26, 2026

Evidence statusApproved product context and qualitative demo research

Update cadenceReview quarterly and after material distributor policy changes

Current distributor documents and portal instructions govern.

The readiness checklist

Eight domains. Open each one and check yourself honestly.

1

Order intake

Can you receive, acknowledge, and track retailer-issued POs reliably?

+
  • A defined intake path for EDI, email, and portal orders
  • An acknowledgement owner and deadline for every PO
  • One place where order status is actually true
2

Routing and compliance

Do you know each distributor’s rules before anything ships?

+
  • Current routing guides and fee schedules on file, with revision dates
  • Appointment, labeling, and pallet requirements checked before pickup
  • A named owner for tracking rule changes
3

Fulfillment coordination

Can your team, 3PL, and carrier work from the same information?

+
  • A shared record connecting the PO, warehouse, and shipment
  • ASN and shipping documents produced on time, every time
  • Exceptions surfaced before the truck leaves, not after
4

Documentation and evidence

Will you be able to prove what happened months later?

+
  • BOLs, PODs, packing lists, and photos retained and findable by PO
  • Appointment and carrier-event history preserved
  • Email decisions connected to the orders they concern
5

Deduction ownership

Who investigates when payment arrives short?

+
  • A named owner for deduction review, not a shared inbox
  • A working distinction between contractual passthrough and disputable charges
  • Dispute deadlines tracked, with time reserved to file
6

Cash planning

Can you carry wholesale payment cycles and deduction risk?

+
  • Payment terms and expected deduction rates modeled before launch
  • Remittances matched to POs so cash variance is explained
  • Trade spend planned separately from unplanned deductions
7

Systems architecture

Do your systems cover the new work, or just the old work?

+
  • A clear map of what ERP, EDI, WMS, email, and portals each own
  • A decision on where the operational story lives across them
  • No workflow that depends on one person’s memory
8

Team ownership

Is the work assigned, or just assumed?

+
  • Every stage of PO-to-cash has a named owner
  • Coverage for vacations, turnover, and volume spikes
  • A plan for capacity that is not only additional headcount

The pattern behind every domain.

Each readiness gap has the same shape: information that exists somewhere is not connected to the purchase order that needs it, at the moment someone must act. Brands that solve the connection problem early avoid retrofitting it during their busiest quarter.

Frequently asked

When should a DTC brand start preparing for wholesale?+

Before the first distributor PO arrives. Routing rules, documentation habits, and deduction ownership are far easier to establish before volume exists than to retrofit under pressure.

What catches new wholesale brands off guard most often?+

Deductions and evidence. Payment arrives short months after shipment, and the documents needed to explain or dispute the difference are scattered across email, the 3PL, and carrier systems, if they were kept at all.

Do we need new systems before entering wholesale?+

Not necessarily. Most brands enter wholesale with the ERP, EDI, and 3PL relationships they already have. The gap is usually coordination and evidence across those systems, not the systems themselves.

How does EasyOps relate to this checklist?+

EasyOps is built around the retailer-issued PO as the shared operational record: intake, coordination, requirements, evidence, and deduction workflow in one place. It complements ERP, WMS, EDI, email, and portals rather than replacing them.
